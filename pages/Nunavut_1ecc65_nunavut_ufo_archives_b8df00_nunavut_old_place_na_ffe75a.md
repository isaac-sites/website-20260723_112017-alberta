## Introduction

One of the easiest ways to miss historical UFO reports from what is now [Nunavut]({{ 'what-really-lies-behind-nunavuts-ufo/' | relative_url }}) is to search only for modern community names. Most Canadian federal UFO files were created before Nunavut became a separate territory on 1 April 1999, when its communities were administered as part of the Northwest Territories. In addition, several settlements were officially renamed during the twentieth century. As a result, archival records may describe the same place under different names, spellings or administrative labels. Searching only for "Iqaluit" or "Nunavut" can therefore overlook reports filed decades earlier under names such as "Frobisher Bay" or broader regional descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CanadaCanada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown - Canada.caMarch 2, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why Frobisher Bay deserves special attention

The strongest example is Iqaluit.

The community was officially known as Frobisher Bay until the restoration of the Inuit name Iqaluit in 1987. Canada's geographical names database records both "Frobisher" and "Frobisher Bay" as previously official settlement names associated with today's city.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geonames.nrcan.gc.ca/search-place-names/unique?id=33e24964c6ce11d892e2080020a0f4c9&amp;wbdisable=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geonames.nrcan.gc.ca">[GeoNames]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geonames.nrcan.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geo Names Place names</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Place names - Iqaluit...</span></span></span>

This creates several potential sources of confusion when searching [UFO archives]({{ 'ufo-archives/' | relative_url }}):


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A document may refer to **Frobisher Bay** meaning the settlement.
* Another may refer to **Frobisher Bay** meaning the large geographic bay.
* Some records simply use **Frobisher**.
* Later references may instead use **Iqaluit**.

</div>

Searching only one version risks missing records indexed under another. This is particularly important because Library and Archives Canada indexes reports largely according to the location stated in the original document rather than applying modern place-name standards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CanadaCanada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown - Canada.caMarch 2, 2026</span></span></span>

## Spelling variants and broad regional labels

Historical UFO files often use geographic descriptions rather than community names, especially when reports came from aircraft, weather stations or remote camps.

Useful alternative searches include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* **Baffin Island**
* **Eastern Arctic**
* **Arctic Archipelago**
* **Northwest Territories**
* **Hudson Bay coast**
* **Foxe Basin**
* **Boothia Peninsula**

</div>

These labels may describe an observation whose nearest modern community lies within Nunavut, even though the territory itself is never mentioned because it did not yet exist. Library and Archives Canada also notes that only about half of its UFO documents identify a specific location, making broader geographic searches worthwhile.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CanadaCanada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown - Canada.caMarch 2, 2026</span></span></span>

Spelling inconsistencies are another obstacle. Older typed reports, telegrams and handwritten notes may abbreviate community names, omit punctuation or use locally accepted spellings that were never formal standards. Because many catalogue searches require close textual matches, trying several variants often produces additional results.

## Building a practical search list

A reliable workflow is to search progressively rather than relying on a single keyword.

For each modern Nunavut community:



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Nunavut_1ecc65_nunavut_ufo_archives_b8df00_nunavut_old_place_na_ffe75a-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Old Place Names illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Nunavut_1ecc65_nunavut_ufo_archives_b8df00_nunavut_old_place_na_ffe75a-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Nunavut_1ecc65_nunavut_ufo_archives_b8df00_nunavut_old_place_na_ffe75a-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Search the current community name.
2. Search every former official name.
3. Search "Northwest Territories" together with the historical name.
4. Search the wider geographic region if the community search produces few results.
5. Browse records by department when location searches fail, particularly Department of Transport, Department of National Defence and RCMP collections, since reports may have been filed according to the receiving agency rather than the observation site. [Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html)

</div>

For example, someone researching historical sightings around present-day Kinngait should search:

* Kinngait
* Cape Dorset [* Northwest Territories + Cape Dorset](#endnote-21 "Endnote 21")
* Baffin Island
* Eastern Arctic

The same principle applies to Iqaluit by searching both "Iqaluit" and "Frobisher Bay", then expanding to broader regional terms if necessary.

## Why historical names matter more than modern maps

Modern digital maps can unintentionally give the impression that historical records should use today's geography. Canada's federal UFO archive does not work that way. The files preserve the administrative and geographic language used when reports were submitted, sometimes decades before Nunavut was created.

Library and Archives Canada itself encourages researchers working with historical collections to search both current and historical community names because archival descriptions frequently retain the terminology used at the time the records were created. Although that guidance is published for broader archival research rather than specifically for UFO files, the same archival principle applies when tracing northern sightings. [Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/indigenous-history/project-naming.html)

For anyone investigating Nunavut's UFO history, the most effective search list is therefore a historical one. Using former settlement names alongside modern community names substantially increases the chance of locating reports that otherwise remain hidden behind the language of an earlier era.
