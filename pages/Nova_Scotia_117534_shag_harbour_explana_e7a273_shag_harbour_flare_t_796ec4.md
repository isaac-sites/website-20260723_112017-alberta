## Introduction

The flare hypothesis is one of the most plausible conventional [explanations]({{ 'explanations-8fddeb/' | relative_url }}) for the lights reported off [Shag Harbour]({{ 'shag-harbour/' | relative_url }}), Nova Scotia, on 4 October 1967. Marine distress flares and military parachute illumination flares can appear as bright orange or yellow lights, descend slowly, flicker in brightness, drift with the wind and seem to hover over water. Those characteristics overlap with several features described by witnesses, making flares a reasonable possibility to investigate rather than dismiss outright.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theblackarchive.net/en/case/013" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackarchive.net">[검은 기록 보관소]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackarchive.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">검은 기록 보관소The Shag Harbour UFO Incident — CASE FILE 013 &#124; The Black Archive</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">검은 기록 보관소The Shag Harbour UFO Incident — CASE FILE 013 &#124; The Black Archive</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that no evidence has emerged showing that flares were actually used in the area that night. More than half a century later, the flare explanation remains a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated solution. It explains some reported observations well but leaves important evidential gaps, particularly the absence of a documented launch, the reported formation of multiple lights and the [official search]({{ 'official-search/' | relative_url }}) that initially treated the event as something other than known illumination devices.

## Were the reported lights consistent with parachute flares?

Parachute flares are designed to illuminate large areas. Suspended beneath a small parachute, they descend slowly while burning brightly, often producing a yellow, orange or white light. From a distance, particularly at night over open water, they can appear almost stationary because their downward movement is gradual while the parachute drifts with the wind. Variations in flame intensity can also create an irregular flickering effect.

For these reasons, flares remain one of the strongest conventional mechanisms proposed by sceptical investigators. Unlike a meteor, they can persist for several minutes after reaching low altitude and may appear to rest above or on the sea before burning out.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theblackarchive.net/en/case/013" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackarchive.net">[검은 기록 보관소]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackarchive.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">검은 기록 보관소The Shag Harbour UFO Incident — CASE FILE 013 &#124; The Black Archive</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">검은 기록 보관소The Shag Harbour UFO Incident — CASE FILE 013 &#124; The Black Archive</span></span></span>

However, reproducing some features of the reports does not establish that flares were actually present. The question is not whether flares *could* look similar, but whether there is evidence that they *were* used off south-west [Nova Scotia]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-nova-scotias/' | relative_url }}) on that particular night.

## Why investigators looked at flares but did not confirm them

The possibility of flares was considered during the original investigation because it represented an obvious conventional explanation for lights descending over coastal waters.

Contemporary government documentation shows that investigators checked whether the reports could have resulted from known causes, including aircraft, floats and flares. One surviving official report states that preliminary enquiries had eliminated those possibilities before the naval investigation proceeded. Although historians debate exactly how conclusive that wording should be interpreted, it demonstrates that flares were not ignored—they were actively examined during the initial response.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://shagharbourincident.wordpress.com/category/updates/documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shagharbourincident.wordpress.com">[Shag Harbour UFO Incident]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shagharbourincident.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO Incident Documents – Shag Harbour UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO IncidentDocuments – Shag Harbour UFO IncidentJuly 6, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 6, 2014</span></span></span>

Modern sceptical writers have noted that other official communications are more cautious, stating only that no evidence supporting a conventional explanation was found rather than explicitly proving that flares were impossible. This distinction matters. A lack of supporting evidence is not the same as positive proof that no flare was involved, but neither does it confirm the flare hypothesis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theblackarchive.net/en/case/013" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackarchive.net">[검은 기록 보관소]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackarchive.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">검은 기록 보관소The Shag Harbour UFO Incident — CASE FILE 013 &#124; The Black Archive</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">검은 기록 보관소The Shag Harbour UFO Incident — CASE FILE 013 &#124; The Black Archive</span></span></span>

## The missing launch record

The principal weakness of the flare explanation is evidential rather than technical.

No confirmed military exercise, naval operation, Coast Guard activity or civilian vessel has been identified as having launched parachute or distress flares in the vicinity of Shag Harbour at the relevant time. Despite decades of investigation by researchers and repeated examination of Canadian government records, no documented source for such flares has emerged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://decur.org/cases/shag-harbour-1967" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: decur.org">[DECUR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">decur.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour Incident - DECUR...</span></span></span>

This absence does not prove that no flare was fired. Records can be incomplete, and a privately owned vessel could theoretically have discharged a distress flare without leaving surviving documentation. Nevertheless, the hypothesis requires an unidentified launch whose existence has never been independently established.

For historians, this creates an important distinction between possibility and evidence. The physical behaviour of flares fits some witness descriptions, but the historical record has yet to identify the people, vessel or operation responsible for launching them.

## The formation problem

[Witnesses]({{ 'witnesses-3e07e4/' | relative_url }}) did not consistently describe a single isolated light.

Several early accounts refer to multiple lights—often four—arranged in a roughly horizontal line before the apparent descent. While eyewitness testimony varied in both colour and number of lights, reports of a small formation appear frequently enough to require explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://shagharbourincident.wordpress.com/category/updates/documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shagharbourincident.wordpress.com">[Shag Harbour UFO Incident]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shagharbourincident.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO Incident Documents – Shag Harbour UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO IncidentDocuments – Shag Harbour UFO IncidentJuly 6, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 6, 2014</span></span></span>

Multiple flares are certainly possible. Military aircraft can release several illumination flares in sequence, and multiple distress flares may also be fired. Yet the formation introduces additional questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Why would several flares appear together over that location?
* What aircraft or vessel launched them?
* Why has no corresponding operation been identified?
* Why were no participants later connected to the incident?

</div>

Without answers to those questions, the formation remains another unresolved aspect of the flare theory rather than evidence in its favour.

## What the flare theory explains—and what it does not

The flare explanation performs well when restricted to the visible lights themselves.

It naturally accounts for:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Slow apparent descent.
* Bright orange or yellow colours.
* Flickering illumination.
* Apparent hovering over the sea.
* A gradual disappearance as the flare burned out.

</div>

Its explanatory power becomes weaker when additional reported features are included.

Some witnesses and responders described yellow foam on the water, while later narratives connected the incident with prolonged search operations and an apparent impact location. Whether every one of these reported elements originated from the same event is uncertain, and several may have become linked only after the initial emergency response began.
