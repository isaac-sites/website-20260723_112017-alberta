## Introduction

[Manitoba]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-manitobas-ufo/' | relative_url }}) UFO reports were split across several agencies because Canada never created a single permanent office responsible for investigating unidentified flying objects. Instead, reports moved through existing government organisations according to their normal responsibilities. A sighting near an airport might be treated as an aviation matter, an unusual event near a military installation as a defence concern, an alleged landing as a police investigation, and an unexplained light in the sky as something for scientific review. This meant that one Manitoba incident could generate several separate files without any organisation producing a single definitive conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[ISED Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISED CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

The result was a fragmented paper trail rather than a coordinated investigative system. For readers exploring Manitoba's UFO history, this explains why important cases such as [Falcon Lake]({{ 'falcon-lake/' | relative_url }}) appear repeatedly in different archival collections and why many reports remained officially "unresolved" in the sense that no agency was tasked with issuing a final national judgement.

## Which agencies received Manitoba reports?

Rather than following a dedicated UFO reporting chain, Manitoba cases entered whichever organisation first became aware of them.

## Why policing, defence and science had different priorities

The division of responsibility reflected government functions rather than competing theories about UFOs.

### Police focused on events on the ground

When Manitoba witnesses claimed that an object had landed, caused [injuries]({{ 'injuries/' | relative_url }}) or left physical traces, police naturally became involved. Their role was not to determine whether an object was extraterrestrial but to establish basic facts:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* whether anyone had been harmed;
* whether property had been damaged;
* whether fraud or misidentification might be involved;
* whether witnesses appeared credible.

</div>

These are routine policing questions and explain why RCMP files often contain interviews, sketches and site descriptions instead of technical analysis.

### Defence asked whether Canada faced a security problem

Military authorities approached reports differently. Their concern was whether an unidentified object represented:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an aircraft entering controlled airspace;
* a possible national security issue;
* unusual military activity;
* something requiring air-defence awareness.

</div>

If a report clearly posed no defence concern, the military often had little reason to continue an extensive investigation, even if the witness remained convinced something extraordinary had occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[ISED Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISED CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

### Scientists looked for ordinary explanations

After responsibility shifted to the NRC in 1967, scientific review became more systematic but remained limited in scope.

Dr Peter Millman and colleagues generally attempted to determine whether observations could be explained by known astronomical or atmospheric phenomena, including:

* bright planets;
* meteors;
* satellites;
* optical effects;
* ordinary aircraft.

The NRC was not established as a forensic investigation service. It largely evaluated reports that had already been gathered elsewhere, meaning many Manitoba files consisted of correspondence and assessments rather than field investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[ISED Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISED CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why Manitoba cases were duplicated

The same report frequently appeared several times because information travelled between agencies instead of remaining within one office.

A typical sequence might look like this:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. A witness contacted local police.
2. Police informed the RCMP or another federal authority.
3. If aviation appeared relevant, the Department of Transport became involved.
4. Defence officials reviewed whether military interests were affected.
5. Copies eventually reached the NRC for scientific assessment.

</div>

Each organisation created its own file, often containing overlapping witness statements, letters or summaries. Since no single department controlled the entire process, duplicate documentation accumulated naturally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[ISED Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISED CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

This also explains why historians sometimes discover slightly different versions of the same Manitoba incident in separate archival collections.

## Why no single agency issued final verdicts

One of the most misunderstood aspects of Canadian UFO records is the absence of formal government conclusions.

This did not necessarily mean officials believed a case was mysterious. More often, it reflected administrative limits:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* one department finished the part of the investigation relevant to its mandate;
* another department received only copies rather than original evidence;
* no organisation possessed authority to produce an official nationwide ruling;
* once immediate public safety or defence concerns disappeared, active investigation usually ended.

</div>

The Sky Canada Project's historical review notes that federal responsibility shifted repeatedly between departments over several decades before the NRC stopped collecting reports in 1995. The 1967 Falcon Lake incident illustrates the fragmented process particularly well.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ys-D0FaIXok" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: youtube.com">[youtube.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">youtube.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Falcon Lake UFO Incident (MB</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Falcon Lake, Manitoba UFO Incident of 1967 - Canada&#x27;s Most Famous ET Encounter...</span></span></span>

Stefan Michalak's report involved alleged physical injuries, claims of a landed object and material collected from the site. Those circumstances attracted attention from several organisations at once:

* police documented Michalak's account and injuries;
* the RCMP conducted interviews and searches;

No organisation directed every stage from beginning to end. ## What this means for Manitoba's UFO archives today

The fragmented structure is one reason Manitoba occupies an important place in Canada's UFO documentary record. Researchers can compare police reports, defence memoranda, transport records and scientific correspondence to reconstruct how officials responded, but they must often combine material from several archival series.

This arrangement has two important consequences for interpreting the evidence:

* **Multiple government files do not necessarily indicate multiple investigations.** Often they reflect the circulation of the same report through different bureaucracies.
* **An unresolved file is not equivalent to official endorsement.** In many cases it simply means no agency possessed either the responsibility or the evidence needed to issue a definitive explanation.

Understanding that administrative structure helps explain why Manitoba's UFO history contains such rich archival documentation while also lacking a single authoritative government judgement on many of its most famous cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[ISED Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISED CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
