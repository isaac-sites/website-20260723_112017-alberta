## Introduction

The Stanton T. Friedman fonds at the Provincial Archives of [New Brunswick]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-new-brunswicks/' | relative_url }}) is one of the largest private UFO research collections ever donated to a Canadian public archive. Its preservation is difficult not because of the subject matter alone, but because of the collection's sheer size, mixed formats and highly disordered state. Archivists have described receiving roughly five cargo vans of material from Friedman's Fredericton home, containing everything from correspondence and government records to audio tapes, photographs, artefacts and personal memorabilia. Turning that accumulation into a usable archival collection is a long-term process that involves far more than placing boxes on shelves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://activehistory.ca/blog/2020/05/12/archivists-in-isolation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: activehistory.ca">[activehistory.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">activehistory.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Active History Archivists in Isolation – Active History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Active HistoryArchivists in Isolation – Active HistoryMay 12, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2020</span></span></span>

For New Brunswick, this work has broader significance. The collection documents decades of Canadian and international UFO research carried out from Fredericton, making it both a provincial historical resource and an unusually complex preservation project.

## What arrived in the five cargo vans

The biggest challenge began with the condition of the donation itself. According to staff at the Provincial Archives of New Brunswick, the Friedman fonds filled approximately five cargo vans and occupied several rooms during processing. Archivists estimated that what was visible in one processing room represented only a fraction of the total collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://activehistory.ca/blog/2020/05/12/archivists-in-isolation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: activehistory.ca">[Active History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">activehistory.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Active History Archivists in Isolation – Active History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Active HistoryArchivists in Isolation – Active HistoryMay 12, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2020</span></span></span>

Unlike many archival donations that arrive with some existing filing system, Friedman's working papers reflected decades of active research. Materials included:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* handwritten notes and research files;
* correspondence with witnesses, researchers and journalists;
* lecture notes, travel itineraries and speaking records;

* audio cassettes and video recordings;
* copies of government records obtained through freedom of information requests;
* manuscripts, draft articles and book material;
* physical objects including promotional items, artwork and unusual artefacts collected during investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://activehistory.ca/blog/2020/05/12/archivists-in-isolation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: activehistory.ca">[Active History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">activehistory.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Active History Archivists in Isolation – Active History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Active HistoryArchivists in Isolation – Active HistoryMay 12, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2020</span></span></span>

## How archivists organise mixed and disordered records

One common misconception is that archives simply catalogue donated material. In reality, large personal collections must first be intellectually reconstructed before researchers can use them.

Archivists processing the Friedman fonds have publicly described it as "chaotic" and the greatest challenge of one archivist's two-decade career. Their work involves identifying how records relate to one another, separating unrelated material, preserving the original context where possible, and creating descriptions that allow future researchers to understand how Friedman actually worked rather than merely what subjects interested him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://activehistory.ca/blog/2020/05/12/archivists-in-isolation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: activehistory.ca">[Active History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">activehistory.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Active History Archivists in Isolation – Active History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Active HistoryArchivists in Isolation – Active HistoryMay 12, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2020</span></span></span>

This arrangement process generally includes several distinct tasks:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* identifying duplicate material without destroying useful research evidence;
* separating personal papers from professional research records;
* grouping correspondence into coherent series;
* rehousing fragile documents in preservation-quality folders and boxes;
* identifying obsolete or deteriorating audiovisual formats that require specialised handling;
* recording enough descriptive metadata to produce reliable finding aids.

</div>

These tasks require professional judgement because archives normally preserve a creator's original order whenever it still exists. When that order has largely broken down—as appears to have happened with portions of the Friedman collection—archivists must carefully reconstruct relationships without introducing misleading new structures. This work also supports the Provincial Archives' legal responsibility to classify, catalogue and preserve historically significant records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://laws.gnb.ca/en/document/cs/a-11.1/20171011" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: laws.gnb.ca">[laws.gnb.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">laws.gnb.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">&gt;</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">&gt;October 11, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 11, 2017</span></span></span>

## Mixed formats create different preservation problems

The fonds is difficult because it is not a paper archive alone.

Different media age in different ways. Paper may require conservation treatment for tears or acidic deterioration. Magnetic audio and videotapes can suffer from chemical breakdown and obsolete playback equipment. Photographic materials require different storage environments from printed documents, while three-dimensional artefacts must often be stored separately from paper records.

Each format therefore follows its own preservation workflow rather than a single standard process. The result is that one accession can involve expertise ranging from paper conservation to audiovisual preservation and digital migration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://activehistory.ca/blog/2020/05/12/archivists-in-isolation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: activehistory.ca">[Active History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">activehistory.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Active History Archivists in Isolation – Active History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Active HistoryArchivists in Isolation – Active HistoryMay 12, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2020</span></span></span>

The unusual subject matter also introduces practical cataloguing challenges. Files may concern well-known cases such as Roswell, Canadian sightings, lecture tours, media interviews and correspondence with other UFO investigators, all of which need accurate description so future researchers can distinguish evidence, opinion, publicity and administrative records.

## Why digitisation does not make everything instantly accessible

A frequent assumption is that scanning a collection immediately makes it available online. The Friedman fonds illustrates why that is rarely true.

Digitisation comes only after archivists know what they possess and how individual records relate to one another. Scanning unidentified or poorly organised material simply creates large numbers of digital files with little research value.

Before digitisation can proceed efficiently, archivists generally need to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* identify and arrange the records;
* create accurate descriptions and metadata;
* determine copyright and access restrictions where necessary;
* assess the condition of fragile originals;
* decide which formats require specialised digital capture.

</div>

New Brunswick's digitisation standards likewise emphasise planning, quality control and metadata rather than treating scanning as a substitute for archival processing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gnb.ca/en/gov/information-management/digitize-records/digitization-standard.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gnb.ca">[gnb.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gnb.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Digitization Standard</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Digitization Standard</span></span></span>

Even after digitisation, many archival collections continue to require in-person consultation because not every item can be placed online, particularly where copyright, privacy or preservation concerns apply.

## Why the challenge matters for New Brunswick's UFO history

The difficulty of preserving the Stanton Friedman fonds reflects its research value rather than simply its size. Friedman's Fredericton office functioned as the operational centre of an international research career spanning decades. The collection captures not only famous UFO controversies but also the everyday mechanics of investigation: correspondence, document requests, lecture preparation, media engagement and debate among researchers.

Because the material arrived as a massive, mixed and partially disordered working archive, processing it is necessarily slow. The result is that access improves gradually as archivists complete arrangement and description rather than all at once.

For New Brunswick, the preservation effort ensures that one of Canada's most significant privately assembled UFO research collections can eventually be studied as a historical record. Whether readers ultimately agree with Friedman's conclusions or remain sceptical of them, the archive provides an unusually detailed record of how modern UFO research was conducted from Fredericton over several decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://activehistory.ca/blog/2020/05/12/archivists-in-isolation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: activehistory.ca">[activehistory.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">activehistory.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Active History Archivists in Isolation – Active History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Active HistoryArchivists in Isolation – Active HistoryMay 12, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2020</span></span></span>
