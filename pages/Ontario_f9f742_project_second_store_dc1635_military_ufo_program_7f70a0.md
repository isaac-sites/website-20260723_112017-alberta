## Introduction

Project [Second Storey]({{ 'second-storey-c502dd/' | relative_url }}) was not created to prove or disprove extraordinary claims. Its purpose was much narrower: to determine whether reports of unidentified objects over Canada justified a sustained military investigation or represented a genuine defence problem. Working from Ottawa between 1952 and 1954 under the Defence Research Board, the committee examined whether "flying saucer" reports pointed to hostile aircraft, intelligence threats or scientifically significant phenomena. Its conclusion was cautious but clear. The evidence available did not meet the threshold for a permanent armed-services programme, and the Canadian military should not devote substantial resources to investigating UFO reports. Instead, officials recommended maintaining a limited reporting system so that any genuinely important case could still be identified if one emerged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

For [Ontario]({{ 'what-does-ontarios-ufo-record-really/' | relative_url }})'s UFO history, this decision is significant because it was reached in Ottawa by the federal institutions responsible for Canada's military science and defence policy. It shaped how Canadian authorities handled unexplained aerial reports for decades afterwards, favouring observation and record-keeping over an active military investigation.

## What counted as a defence concern?

Project Second Storey evaluated reports through a Cold War security lens rather than a search for extraterrestrial visitors. Committee members asked practical questions:

* Could a sighting represent an unknown military aircraft?
* Did it indicate a weakness in Canadian air-defence awareness?
* Was there evidence of foreign reconnaissance or weapons testing?
* Could the report be investigated using reliable scientific methods?

</div>

This approach meant that unusual appearance alone was never enough to justify military involvement. A report had to contain sufficiently detailed, verifiable information to indicate a possible threat to Canadian sovereignty or airspace. Most reports failed this test because they consisted of brief visual observations without corroborating radar returns, physical evidence or consistent witness accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

The committee therefore separated two different questions that are often merged in popular discussions:

* whether something remained unidentified; and
* whether it represented a defence danger.

An unexplained observation did not automatically become a military concern simply because no immediate explanation was available.

## Why the evidence failed the threshold

The committee's rejection of a full military programme reflected both the quality of the available reports and the practical demands of defence planning.

Most cases suffered from familiar investigative problems:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* observations lasting only a few seconds;
* uncertain estimates of distance, altitude and speed;
* conflicting witness descriptions;
* insufficient meteorological or astronomical information;
* no recoverable physical evidence.

</div>

Without dependable data, investigators could rarely distinguish between genuinely unusual events and ordinary objects seen under unusual conditions. Peter Millman, the committee's chair, consistently argued that many reports were better explained by astronomical objects, meteors or other natural phenomena than by unknown aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canadaufohistory.com/1947-1954/project-second-storey" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadaufohistory.com">[Canada UFO History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadaufohistory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada UFO HistoryCanada UFO History - Project Second Storey...</span></span></span>

The committee also concluded that the phenomenon did not readily support systematic scientific investigation. Reports arrived unpredictably, could not be reproduced under controlled conditions and usually lacked measurements that researchers could analyse objectively. Rather than revealing a coherent pattern, the accumulated cases remained highly variable and resistant to firm conclusions. The committee eventually stated that the subject did not "lend itself to a scientific method of investigation," an assessment that became one of the defining reasons for ending the project.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canadaufohistory.com/1947-1954/project-second-storey" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadaufohistory.com">[Canada UFO History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadaufohistory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada UFO HistoryCanada UFO History - Project Second Storey...</span></span></span>

Importantly, this was not the same as declaring every report explained. Instead, officials judged that the remaining unexplained cases were too poorly documented to justify expanding military resources.

## Why a permanent military programme was considered unnecessary

By 1953 the committee had effectively answered the question that justified its creation. It found no persuasive evidence that unidentified sightings represented an organised threat requiring continuous armed-services investigation.

Several considerations supported that judgement:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **No demonstrated hostile capability.** Reports had not revealed aircraft displaying behaviour that clearly indicated an enemy weapon or reconnaissance system threatening Canada.
* **Limited defence value.** Extensive investigations were unlikely to improve national security when the overwhelming majority of reports produced no actionable intelligence.
* **Resource priorities.** During the Cold War, military intelligence already faced genuine strategic responsibilities. Officials judged that sustained UFO investigations would divert personnel from higher-priority defence tasks.
* **Scientific uncertainty.** Since most reports lacked objective evidence, additional investigators alone were unlikely to produce better answers.

</div>

Meeting records show that the committee decided a large-scale investigation by the Canadian services was not warranted, even while acknowledging that reports should continue to be collected centrally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

This distinction is often overlooked. The committee did not conclude that every future report would necessarily be trivial. Rather, it concluded that the available evidence did not justify creating an enduring military organisation devoted to the subject.

## What collection continued after the decision

Ending an active military investigation did not end government interest altogether.

Instead of recommending complete abandonment, Project Second Storey favoured limited monitoring. The committee developed a standard reporting questionnaire intended to improve the quality and consistency of reports received by government departments. Although the form never became widely adopted across all agencies, it reflected the committee's belief that better evidence—not more investigators—was the priority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

After Project Second Storey ended in 1954, reports continued to reach organisations such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* the Royal Canadian Mounted Police;
* the Department of National Defence;
* the Department of Transport; and
* local police and other government bodies.

</div>

Responsibility became dispersed rather than concentrated within a dedicated military project. Canada therefore continued collecting information without maintaining a specialised defence investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why the decision mattered for Ontario's UFO history

Although Project Second Storey examined reports from across Canada, its institutional centre was Ottawa. The Defence Research Board, Dominion Observatory and senior defence officials who shaped the decision all operated from Ontario's federal capital.

The committee's conclusions established a model that influenced Canadian policy for decades:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* military investigation would be justified only by credible evidence of a defence threat;
* unexplained reports alone were insufficient to trigger large-scale security operations;
* scientific assessment should rely on standardised, objective reporting wherever possible.

</div>

This governance approach distinguished Canada from more publicly visible American programmes such as Project Blue Book. Rather than building a permanent military organisation around UFO reports, Canadian officials chose a lower-profile system focused on information collection and periodic review.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canadaufohistory.com/1947-1954/project-second-storey" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadaufohistory.com">[Canada UFO History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadaufohistory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada UFO HistoryCanada UFO History - Project Second Storey...</span></span></span>

## A decision based on evidence thresholds, not certainty

One common misunderstanding is that Project Second Storey "proved" there was nothing unusual in Canadian skies. The surviving records support a narrower conclusion.

The committee decided that available reports failed to satisfy two practical thresholds simultaneously:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* they did not demonstrate a credible threat to Canadian defence; and
* they did not provide evidence robust enough to support sustained scientific investigation.

</div>

That distinction explains why the committee rejected a permanent military UFO programme while still accepting that unusual aerial reports should continue to be documented. From a governance perspective, the project was less a verdict on the ultimate nature of UFOs than a judgement about how governments should allocate military resources when evidence remains fragmentary and inconclusive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>
