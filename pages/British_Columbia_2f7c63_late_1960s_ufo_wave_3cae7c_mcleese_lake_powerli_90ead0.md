## Introduction

The McLeese Lake sighting of 26 August 1967 is one of the better-known episodes from [British Columbia]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-british/' | relative_url }})'s late-1960s UFO wave because it combined multiple [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses/' | relative_url }}) with an unusual setting: a glowing red object apparently travelling above newly installed transmission lines. The event is frequently retold as a case in which a UFO was "inspecting" power lines. However, the available evidence supports a more cautious conclusion. Witnesses consistently described an unusual light, but the idea that it was deliberately examining the electrical system rests on interpretation rather than demonstrable physical evidence. The absence of power disruptions, measurable electrical effects or any independent confirmation means the "inspection" claim remains speculative rather than established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufobc.ca/History/1960/1967invasion_v2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufobc.ca">[UFO BC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufobc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1967invasion v2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO BC1967 - The Year We Were InvadedNovember 7, 1969...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 1969</span></span></span>

## What residents reported on 26 August 1967

According to contemporary accounts later reproduced by British Columbia UFO archives, the incident began at around 10 p.m. on a warm, still evening. At least a dozen residents reportedly watched a glowing, pulsating red object approach from the south and move slowly along the route of newly erected transmission cables associated with the future Peace River hydroelectric development. Because the lines crossed a ridge east of McLeese Lake, observers had an unusually clear view for several minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufobc.ca/History/1960/1967invasion_v2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufobc.ca">[UFO BC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufobc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1967invasion v2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO BC1967 - The Year We Were InvadedNovember 7, 1969...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 1969</span></span></span>

Witness descriptions shared several broad features:

* The object appeared as an intense red light that brightened and dimmed rhythmically.
* It travelled slowly enough for multiple people to observe it over roughly three to four minutes.
* No engine noise or other sound was heard, despite one witness deliberately listening after switching off a washing machine.
* One observer thought there was a darker area beneath the light, while another, using binoculars, described a greenish feature near its centre rather than underneath it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufobc.ca/History/1960/1967invasion_v2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufobc.ca">[UFO BC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufobc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1967invasion v2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO BC1967 - The Year We Were InvadedNovember 7, 1969...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 1969</span></span></span>

The differing visual details are not unusual in multiple-witness cases involving distant lights. They neither confirm nor disprove the event but illustrate how observers can agree on the main phenomenon while disagreeing about finer features.

## Why the power-line link seemed persuasive

The suggestion that the object was inspecting the transmission system emerged because witnesses repeatedly remarked that it appeared to remain directly above the line of the cables.

One witness recalled that the object "stayed right over those lines as if it was studying them", wording that later writers often treated almost as a factual description rather than an impression. This interpretation became especially influential because the sighting occurred during a period when new high-voltage infrastructure was transforming the Cariboo region, making the transmission route a striking landmark against the night sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufobc.ca/History/1960/1967invasion_v2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufobc.ca">[UFO BC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufobc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1967invasion v2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO BC1967 - The Year We Were InvadedNovember 7, 1969...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 1969</span></span></span>

Several factors helped the inspection narrative gain traction:

* **A clear visual reference.** Transmission lines running along an exposed ridge created an obvious line against which movement could be judged.
* **A prolonged observation.** Unlike a brief flash, the object remained visible for several minutes, encouraging witnesses to interpret its behaviour.
* **The broader 1967 wave.** Similar reports elsewhere in British Columbia led investigators to look for patterns, increasing the appeal of interpreting the object's apparent path as purposeful rather than coincidental.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufobc.ca/History/1960/1967invasion_v2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufobc.ca">[UFO BC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufobc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1967invasion v2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO BC1967 - The Year We Were InvadedNovember 7, 1969...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 1969</span></span></span>

Importantly, the witnesses reported what they saw—a light apparently following the cables. The further claim that it was deliberately examining the infrastructure is an inference rather than an observation.

## What the absence of electrical effects means

The strongest argument against the inspection claim is the lack of supporting physical evidence.

Accounts consistently note that the transmission line was newly installed and not yet carrying electricity. If that is correct, there could have been no interaction with an energised high-voltage system because none existed at the time. Later writers sometimes argued that the object must therefore have been interested in the physical structure itself, but this simply replaces one assumption with another rather than adding evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufobc.ca/History/1960/1967invasion_v2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufobc.ca">[UFO BC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufobc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1967invasion v2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO BC1967 - The Year We Were InvadedNovember 7, 1969...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 1969</span></span></span>

## How later reporting changed the story

Most detailed modern retellings ultimately derive from a relatively small number of UFO publications and later compilations rather than from extensive contemporary official investigations. John Magor's influential history of the 1967 British Columbia wave preserved valuable witness testimony, but it also interpreted many incidents through an extraterrestrial framework. As a result, descriptive observations and speculative [explanations]({{ 'explanations-89fcc8/' | relative_url }}) sometimes became closely intertwined in later retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufobc.ca/History/1960/1967invasion_v2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufobc.ca">[UFO BC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufobc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1967invasion v2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO BC1967 - The Year We Were InvadedNovember 7, 1969...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 1969</span></span></span>

## Why the case still matters

The McLeese Lake sighting remains historically significant within British Columbia's late-1960s UFO wave because it involved multiple witnesses observing an unusual light for several minutes under conditions that allowed detailed descriptions. It is therefore stronger than many single-observer reports in terms of witness numbers.

However, the specific claim that the object was inspecting power lines is considerably weaker than the underlying sighting itself. The evidence demonstrates that witnesses believed the light tracked the transmission route, but it does not demonstrate intent, interaction with the infrastructure or any measurable physical effect. The distinction is important. The event remains an unresolved observation of an unusual aerial light, while the "power-line inspection" interpretation remains an unverified explanation built upon perceived alignment rather than independent evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufobc.ca/History/1960/1967invasion_v2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufobc.ca">[UFO BC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufobc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1967invasion v2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO BC1967 - The Year We Were InvadedNovember 7, 1969...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 1969</span></span></span>
