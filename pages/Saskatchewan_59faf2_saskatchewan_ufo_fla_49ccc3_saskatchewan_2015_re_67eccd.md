## Introduction

[Saskatchewan]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-saskatchewans/' | relative_url }}) recorded 37 reported UFO [sightings]({{ 'sightings/' | relative_url }}) during 2015, making it one of the province's busiest modern reporting years. At first glance, that increase might appear to indicate a classic UFO "flap" – a concentrated wave of related sightings. However, the available evidence points in a different direction. Rather than documenting one coherent event, the 2015 reports were spread across widely separated communities, involved very different descriptions, and lacked the common features that investigators normally expect from a genuine sighting wave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://globalnews.ca/news/2636162/ufo-sightings-up-in-saskatchewan/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: globalnews.ca">[Global News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">globalnews.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Global News UFO sightings up in Saskatchewan &#124; Globalnews.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Global NewsUFO sightings up in Saskatchewan &#124; Globalnews.caApril 13, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 13, 2016</span></span></span>

## Where the 2015 reports were concentrated

The Canadian UFO Survey recorded 37 Saskatchewan reports for 2015. Most originated from the province's two largest population centres, Regina and Saskatoon, while additional reports came from places including Prince Albert, Star City, Mossbank, Weyburn, Gravelbourg, Elfros and Silton near Regina Beach.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://globalnews.ca/news/2636162/ufo-sightings-up-in-saskatchewan/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: globalnews.ca">[Global News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">globalnews.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Global News UFO sightings up in Saskatchewan &#124; Globalnews.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Global NewsUFO sightings up in Saskatchewan &#124; Globalnews.caApril 13, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 13, 2016</span></span></span>

## Why dispersed sightings do not form one event

Researchers generally reserve the term "flap" for a concentrated series of related reports rather than a busy reporting year.

Several characteristics commonly associated with a genuine flap were absent from Saskatchewan's 2015 increase.

**No clear geographical focus.** Reports were distributed across multiple cities and rural communities rather than clustering around one locality or corridor.

**No tight time window.** The reports occurred throughout the calendar year instead of building rapidly over several days or weeks.

**No consistent object description.** Witnesses described lights, oval objects and other varied appearances rather than repeatedly reporting the same distinctive shape or behaviour.

**No documented chain of corroboration.** Publicly available records do not show multiple independent witnesses describing the same object from different locations, nor is there evidence of supporting radar data, air traffic observations or coordinated police investigations linking the cases together.

Each of these points weakens the argument that Saskatchewan experienced one province-wide event. Instead, the evidence is more consistent with unrelated observations being collected into a single annual total.

## Why a higher total does not necessarily mean more unexplained phenomena

The Canadian UFO Survey has long emphasised that a report enters the database because the witness could not identify what they saw at the time, not because investigators concluded it represented something extraordinary. Reports are gathered from multiple sources, including investigators, media coverage, online submissions and other reporting channels, with varying levels of detail and reliability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canadianuforeport.com/survey/essay/2015essay.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadianuforeport.com">[canadianuforeport.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadianuforeport.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">/.................. \ =====...</span></span></span>

## What evidence would have confirmed a genuine wave?

Had Saskatchewan experienced a true provincial flap during 2015, investigators would expect to find several reinforcing lines of evidence rather than simply a larger annual count.

Examples would include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* numerous reports within the same few days or weeks;
* repeated descriptions of an object with similar appearance and behaviour;
* independent witnesses observing the same event from different locations;
* supporting records from aviation authorities, pilots, police or radar systems;
* photographs or videos that could be linked to the same incident;
* a clear geographical progression suggesting one continuing event.

</div>

None of these elements emerges clearly from the published record for Saskatchewan in 2015. Instead, the year's reports remain a collection of separate incidents with different characteristics and varying evidential strength.

## Why the distinction matters for Saskatchewan's UFO history

Calling 2015 a "flap" risks overstating what the evidence actually shows. Saskatchewan certainly experienced one of its stronger reporting years, and the increase deserves attention because it reflects sustained public reporting across the province. Yet the available data support a more cautious conclusion.

The 37 reports demonstrate that many people submitted observations during 2015, but they do not establish a single coordinated wave of unusual aerial events. Within Saskatchewan's broader history of UFO reports, the year is therefore better understood as a reporting spike than as a documented province-wide flap.
