## Introduction

Many people expect [Saskatchewan]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-saskatchewans/' | relative_url }}) UFO reports made to the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP) to be easy to locate by searching for terms such as "UFO" or "unidentified flying object". In practice, they are often surprisingly difficult to find. The main reason is administrative rather than mysterious: the RCMP has never operated a dedicated national system for collecting, classifying or investigating UFO reports. Instead, unusual aerial [sightings]({{ 'sightings/' | relative_url }}) are logged within ordinary policing systems according to the officer's assessment of the incident, not according to whether a witness believed they saw a UFO. As a result, reports from Saskatchewan can be dispersed across several unrelated incident categories, making later searches incomplete or misleading.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[ISED Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISED CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

This matters when reconstructing Saskatchewan's UFO history. An absence of easily searchable RCMP files does not necessarily mean few reports were made. Equally, the existence of a police occurrence record only demonstrates that someone contacted police and that the matter was recorded or assessed; it does not establish what was actually seen.

## How local police decide whether to open a file

When someone in rural Saskatchewan reports unusual lights or an unidentified object, the RCMP's first question is whether the situation involves public safety or a potential offence. ## Why sightings receive inconsistent incident categories

The largest obstacle to finding RCMP UFO records is inconsistent classification.

The Sky Canada Project, commissioned by the Office of the Chief Science Advisor, found that the RCMP has no formal national policy governing the collection or dissemination of unidentified aerial phenomenon (UAP) reports. Without a dedicated reporting category, call-takers and responding officers classify incidents using existing policing categories that fit the circumstances as they understand them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[ISED Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISED CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

That can produce several outcomes.


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Aeronautics Act-related incidents.** Some reports are entered as non-criminal occurrences connected with aviation or airspace matters.
* **Suspicious vehicle or suspicious activity.** If little is known about the object, the report may be grouped with entirely unrelated public-safety calls.
* **Other operational categories.** Depending on the circumstances, a report may be associated with noise complaints, trespassing, public assistance or another general occurrence type rather than any aviation-related label.

</div>

The consequence is that a database search for the word "UFO" may overlook numerous relevant files because that word never appeared in the original incident coding. The Sky Canada Project concluded that this inconsistency made it impossible for the RCMP to produce meaningful national statistics on UAP reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[ISED Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISED CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

For historians examining Saskatchewan cases decades later, this creates an important limitation. Searching by location, date or associated police occurrence may be more productive than searching by UFO terminology alone.

## What police records can and cannot establish

An RCMP occurrence record can provide valuable evidence, but its evidential value has clear limits.

Police documentation may establish that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a witness reported an event promptly;
* officers attended a location;
* statements were taken;
* photographs or physical observations were recorded;
* another agency was contacted if aviation safety appeared relevant.

</div>

Those records strengthen the historical documentation of a sighting because they show contemporaneous reporting rather than a recollection many years later.

However, police records generally cannot establish:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the identity of an unidentified object;
* whether radar confirmed the observation;
* whether the object represented an aircraft, astronomical phenomenon or something else;
* that an extraordinary explanation is correct.

</div>

Even where officers document physical traces or witness accounts, the file normally records what was observed during a policing response rather than reaching a scientific conclusion about the phenomenon itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[ISED Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISED CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why archival searches can still miss RCMP material

Researchers often assume that all historical RCMP UFO files are gathered into one archive. In reality, several factors complicate searches.

First, records created during routine policing remain organised according to ordinary records-management practices rather than a single UFO collection. Second, older files may have been transferred to Library and Archives Canada under broader record series rather than under descriptive UFO titles. Third, archival descriptions reflect the terminology used when the records were created, so a search using modern terms such as "UAP" may not retrieve older material, while a search for "UFO" may miss files catalogued under unrelated policing subjects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">s UFOs: The search for the unknown</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown - Canada.caMarch 2, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 2, 2026</span></span></span>

Library and Archives Canada does hold thousands of historical Canadian government UFO-related records from departments including the RCMP, but these represent archival government files accumulated between 1947 and the early 1980s rather than a complete or continuously maintained national RCMP reporting system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">s UFOs: The search for the unknown</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada&#x27;s UFOs: The search for the unknown - Canada.caMarch 2, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 2, 2026</span></span></span>

## What this means for Saskatchewan's UFO history

For Saskatchewan researchers, the scarcity of easily identifiable RCMP UFO files should not be interpreted as evidence that few reports were made. Instead, it reflects how Canadian policing systems were designed.

The RCMP's role has been to respond to possible public-safety issues, not to maintain a specialised catalogue of unexplained aerial sightings. Reports were therefore recorded according to operational policing needs, with referrals to aviation or defence authorities where appropriate, rather than being collected into a dedicated UFO archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[ISED Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISED CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

As a result, reconstructing Saskatchewan's official UFO history usually requires combining multiple sources—including RCMP records where available, aviation occurrence databases, local newspapers, witness testimony and archival collections—rather than relying on police files alone. This fragmented record-keeping is an important feature of how Saskatchewan sightings entered official records and explains why many genuine police reports appear to disappear when searched using UFO-related terms alone.
