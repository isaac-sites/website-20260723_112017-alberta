## Introduction

In the early 1950s, as reports of "flying saucers" reached Canadian military, police and government offices, officials recognised a basic problem: different departments collected different kinds of information, making reports difficult to compare. Project [Second Storey]({{ 'second-storey-c502dd/' | relative_url }}), an interdepartmental committee based in Ottawa under the Defence Research Board, responded by creating a standard UFO reporting form. The aim was not to prove or disprove extraordinary claims, but to improve the quality of evidence reaching federal authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Ontario_f9f742_project_second_store_dc1635_ufo_reporting_form_f_3992e1-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Reporting Form illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Ontario_f9f742_project_second_store_dc1635_ufo_reporting_form_f_3992e1-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Ontario_f9f742_project_second_store_dc1635_ufo_reporting_form_f_3992e1-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The form represented one of Canada's earliest attempts to treat unusual aerial reports as a data-collection problem rather than simply a curiosity. Yet it never became the nationwide reporting system its designers envisioned. Limited adoption, uncertain administrative responsibility and the committee's own sceptical conclusions meant that the form saw little practical use before Project Second Storey itself came to an end. That failure shaped how UFO reports were handled in Canada for decades afterwards, including in [Ontario]({{ 'what-does-ontarios-ufo-record-really/' | relative_url }}), where many of the relevant departments and archives were based.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

## What information the form required

The proposed reporting form reflected Peter Millman's view that reliable investigation depended on measurable observations rather than dramatic stories. Instead of inviting witnesses to speculate about alien spacecraft or mysterious technologies, it concentrated on facts that could be checked against astronomical, meteorological or [aviation records]({{ 'aviation-records/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canadaufohistory.com/1947-1954/project-second-storey" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadaufohistory.com">[Canada UFO History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadaufohistory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada UFO HistoryCanada UFO History - Project Second Storey...</span></span></span>

Officials wanted reports to include information such as:

* the exact date and time of the observation;
* the location of both the witness and the object;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the object's apparent direction of travel;
* estimated altitude, speed and angular size;
* weather conditions and visibility;
* the duration of the sighting;
* the number of witnesses;
* whether binoculars, cameras or other instruments were used;
* sketches of the object's appearance and movement; and
* any corroborating evidence from aircraft, radar or other observers where available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canadaufohistory.com/1947-1954/project-second-storey" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadaufohistory.com">[Canada UFO History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadaufohistory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada UFO HistoryCanada UFO History - Project Second Storey...</span></span></span>

The emphasis on structured observations mirrored scientific field reporting rather than intelligence questionnaires. Officials hoped that consistent measurements would make it easier to distinguish astronomical objects, meteors, balloons, aircraft and genuine unknowns.

## How officials expected reports to be compared

Project Second Storey's reporting form was designed to solve a practical administrative problem. During the early Cold War, reports could arrive through the Royal Canadian Mounted Police, the Department of National Defence, the Department of Transport, local police forces or civilian correspondence. Without a common format, comparing cases across agencies was difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

The committee expected each department to collect essentially the same information so that analysts in Ottawa could:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* compare reports from different regions using identical categories;
* identify recurring explanations such as planets, meteors or aircraft;
* detect multiple independent reports of the same event;
* judge the quality of individual witnesses without relying solely on narrative descriptions; and
* determine whether any reports justified further scientific or military attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

</div>

For Ontario, this was especially significant because Ottawa housed the Defence Research Board, Dominion Observatory and several of the federal departments expected to exchange information. The reporting form was intended to support a national process, but its administrative centre lay in Ontario's federal institutions.

## Why the form saw so little use

The reporting form has sometimes been portrayed as evidence of a secret national UFO investigation. The historical record suggests something much less dramatic. The form was produced, but it never became the standard reporting mechanism that its designers envisioned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

Several factors explain why.

### The committee lost confidence in the value of large-scale investigation

As Project Second Storey reviewed available cases between 1952 and 1954, members increasingly concluded that most reports contained too little reliable information to support meaningful scientific analysis. They also found no convincing evidence that the sightings represented a defence threat.

If the underlying reports were generally weak, expanding the reporting system became a lower priority. Rather than recommending a permanent investigative programme, the committee advised ending military involvement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

### No department became the permanent owner

The form depended on cooperation between several organisations rather than belonging to a single agency with clear authority.

Reports might originate with police, military personnel, Transport officials or ordinary members of the public. Without one department responsible for enforcing a common procedure, adoption remained inconsistent. Later Canadian UFO reporting became fragmented across multiple agencies instead of flowing into one national database.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

### Public reporting remained decentralised

Even after Project Second Storey ended, Canadians continued sending reports to whichever organisation they considered appropriate. Some contacted the RCMP, others the Department of Transport, National Defence or local authorities.

Because the standard form never became widely embedded in government practice, much of this later reporting continued in letters, telephone calls and locally produced records rather than through a unified federal questionnaire.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>

## What the failure meant for Canadian UFO records

The limited adoption of the reporting form had long-term consequences that historians continue to note.

Canada eventually accumulated thousands of UFO-related records, but they were dispersed across numerous departments with differing procedures and priorities. Researchers have found that many files contain inconsistent levels of detail because no universally applied reporting standard survived beyond the early 1950s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://batadora.trentu.ca/objects/etd-776" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: batadora.trentu.ca">[Batadora]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">batadora.trentu.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">etd 776</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A History of Canada&#x27;s UFO Investigation, 1950-1995 &#124; Digital Collections...</span></span></span>

This fragmentation also complicates comparisons between cases. Some reports contain detailed witness interviews and technical observations, while others preserve little more than brief correspondence. The uneven quality of the archive reflects administrative history as much as the quality of the sightings themselves.

## Why the reporting form still matters

The reporting form's historical importance lies less in how often it was used than in what it reveals about Canadian policy.

Project Second Storey did not assume that every unexplained aerial report deserved a major investigation. Instead, it argued that any serious assessment required consistent, objective observations before conclusions could be drawn. The proposed questionnaire embodied that philosophy by prioritising measurable facts over speculation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canadaufohistory.com/1947-1954/project-second-storey" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canadaufohistory.com">[Canada UFO History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canadaufohistory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada UFO HistoryCanada UFO History - Project Second Storey...</span></span></span>

Its failure illustrates an equally important lesson. Designing a better reporting system is easier than persuading multiple organisations to adopt it. Without sustained institutional support, a carefully constructed form remained little more than a proposal, leaving Canada with a patchwork of records rather than the comprehensive national dataset that Second Storey's members had initially hoped to create. For Ontario, where the committee met and many of the surviving federal archives are located, the reporting form remains an important example of how Canada's earliest official UFO policy focused on evidence collection rather than confirmation of extraordinary claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Innovation CanadaManagement of Public Reporting of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in CanadaJune 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2025</span></span></span>
