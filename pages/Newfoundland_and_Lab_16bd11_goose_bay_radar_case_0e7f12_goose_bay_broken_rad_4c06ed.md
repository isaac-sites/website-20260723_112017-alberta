## Introduction

The [Goose Bay radar]({{ 'goose-bay-radar/' | relative_url }}) incident is often remembered as an unexplained radar contact from late 1948, but the central problem is more specific: the reported track could not be reconstructed into a single, internally consistent flight path. Rather than demonstrating extraordinary performance, the surviving records show that the range, bearing, timing and disappearance point did not fit together when investigators later attempted to plot them. That failure of reconstruction is one of the strongest reasons the case remains unresolved within the UFO history of [Newfoundland and Labrador]({{ 'newfoundland-and-labrador/' | relative_url }}). It illustrates how a genuine radar observation can still resist interpretation if the underlying measurements are incomplete or contradictory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/goose-bay-labrador-october-1948-incident-number-illegible-28931625" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">goose bay labrador october 1948 incident number illegible 28931625</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Goose Bay, Labrador, October 1948 - Incident Number: [ILLEGIBLE] · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1948</span></span></span>

## What the operators reported

According to the surviving Project Blue Book file, two Ground Controlled Approach (GCA) radar operators at Goose Bay observed an unidentified radar return during the evening of 1 November 1948, although the file is catalogued under October 1948 and later publications have repeated several different dates. The target reportedly first appeared about 3¾ miles north-east of the radar site, remained visible for just over four minutes, travelled roughly four miles, and was estimated to move at about 60 miles per hour before disappearing from the display, which was operating on a 7½-mile range. There was no confirmed visual observation to accompany the radar contact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/goose-bay-labrador-october-1948-incident-number-illegible-28931625" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">goose bay labrador october 1948 incident number illegible 28931625</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Goose Bay, Labrador, October 1948 - Incident Number: [ILLEGIBLE] · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1948</span></span></span>

Those figures appear straightforward in isolation. The difficulty emerged only when investigators tried to convert the operators' notes into a physical route across the ground. Unlike modern digital radar recordings, the 1948 system produced no automatic electronic track that could later be replayed or analysed. Reconstruction depended entirely on the reported estimates.

## Where the plotted geometry failed

The key finding from the follow-up investigation was that the reported measurements could not all describe the same movement.

When Newfoundland Base Command personnel examined the case in January 1949, they attempted to plot the reported:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* starting position;
* direction of travel;
* distance covered;
* estimated speed;
* observation time; and
* point where the target disappeared.

</div>

The resulting geometry failed. If the starting point and bearings were accepted, the reported distance and speed did not place the target at the stated disappearance point. If the reported disappearance point was treated as correct, then either the speed, the travel distance or the earlier position estimates had to be wrong. The available figures could not all be true simultaneously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/goose-bay-labrador-october-1948-incident-number-illegible-28931625" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">goose bay labrador october 1948 incident number illegible 28931625</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Goose Bay, Labrador, October 1948 - Incident Number: [ILLEGIBLE] · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1948</span></span></span>

This was not merely a matter of small rounding differences. The inconsistencies were significant enough that investigators specifically recorded them as a weakness in the original report rather than treating them as acceptable observational error.

## Why the measurements conflicted

The surviving documentation does not identify a single mistake responsible for the contradiction. Instead, several ordinary limitations could have combined.

First, the operators were estimating movement from a short-range GCA radar intended primarily to guide aircraft during landing approaches, not to generate precise scientific records of unusual targets. Positions and headings were typically judged from the radar display rather than stored electronically.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/goose-bay-labrador-october-1948-incident-number-illegible-28931625" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">goose bay labrador october 1948 incident number illegible 28931625</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Goose Bay, Labrador, October 1948 - Incident Number: [ILLEGIBLE] · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1948</span></span></span>

Second, several of the reported quantities were themselves estimates rather than direct measurements. Speed was inferred from apparent movement over time. Distance travelled depended on plotting successive positions. Bearings were read from the display. Small errors in more than one estimate could accumulate until the finished track became impossible to reconcile.

Third, no independent confirmation existed. The case file contains no radar photographs, no simultaneous plot from another radar installation, no aircraft interception, and no visual observation that could anchor the radar data to a known location in the sky. Without those independent reference points, later investigators had little opportunity to resolve conflicting measurements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/goose-bay-labrador-october-1948-incident-number-illegible-28931625" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">goose bay labrador october 1948 incident number illegible 28931625</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Goose Bay, Labrador, October 1948 - Incident Number: [ILLEGIBLE] · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1948</span></span></span>

## What contradictory measurements can and cannot prove

The contradictory geometry is sometimes presented as evidence that the object performed impossible manoeuvres. The surviving records do not support that conclusion.

Instead, the inconsistency shows that the dataset itself cannot uniquely describe a real trajectory. Several possibilities remain compatible with the evidence:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* one or more reported positions may have been estimated inaccurately;
* the reported speed may have been calculated incorrectly;
* the disappearance point may have been remembered or recorded inaccurately;
* the radar return could have represented an atmospheric, equipment or propagation effect rather than a solid object following a simple flight path; or
* the target could have been an ordinary object whose recorded measurements became internally inconsistent during reporting.

</div>

Because the observations cannot be reconstructed into a coherent route, none of these possibilities can be eliminated solely from the surviving radar data. The broken geometry weakens attempts to argue either for an extraordinary craft or for any single conventional explanation.

## Why the broken track matters

Within the wider Goose Bay radar mystery, the failed reconstruction is arguably the most important piece of evidence. It explains why later investigators could neither confirm nor confidently dismiss the incident.

If the reported measurements had formed a consistent track, analysts could have compared that route with weather balloons, aircraft movements, meteorological conditions or other known traffic. Because the plotted route itself failed basic geometric consistency, those comparisons become far less reliable.

This also helps explain why later retellings of the Goose Bay incident sometimes quote different speeds, distances or flight characteristics. Once the original measurements proved mutually incompatible, subsequent authors often emphasised different parts of the record, occasionally mixing this short-range case with separate Goose Bay radar stories involving much higher reported speeds. ## Why the case remains unresolved

The Goose Bay radar incident remains unresolved not because investigators verified an unknown craft, but because the surviving evidence is too inconsistent to reconstruct a reliable radar track. The original observation appears genuine in the sense that [two operators]({{ 'two-operators/' | relative_url }}) reported watching an unusual return on their display. However, the conflicting range, bearing, speed and timing measurements prevent investigators from determining exactly where the target travelled or even whether all recorded figures describe the same object.

For the history of UFO reports in Newfoundland and Labrador, that distinction is significant. Goose Bay is best understood as a case where the limitations of the available measurements—not necessarily the nature of the target itself—created the enduring mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/goose-bay-labrador-october-1948-incident-number-illegible-28931625" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">goose bay labrador october 1948 incident number illegible 28931625</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Goose Bay, Labrador, October 1948 - Incident Number: [ILLEGIBLE] · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: october 1948</span></span></span>
