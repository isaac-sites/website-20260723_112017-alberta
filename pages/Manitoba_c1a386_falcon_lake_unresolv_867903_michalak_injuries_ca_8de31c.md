## Introduction

One reason the 1967 [Falcon Lake]({{ 'falcon-lake/' | relative_url }}) incident remains central to [Manitoba]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-manitobas-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s UFO history is that Stefan Michalak's injuries were real, even though the event he said caused them has never been independently verified. Medical staff treated burns to his body, police encountered him while he was visibly distressed, and investigators from several agencies accepted that he had suffered genuine physical harm. What they could not establish was *why*. More than half a century later, that distinction remains crucial. The documented injuries make Falcon Lake more than a simple eyewitness story, but they do not prove the existence of an unknown craft or any extraordinary mechanism behind the burns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-053.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 1 - Canada.caMay 15, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 15, 2019</span></span></span>

## What doctors and police confirmed

The strongest evidence in this aspect of the Falcon Lake case concerns Michalak's physical condition rather than the explanation he offered for it.

On the afternoon of 20 May 1967, Michalak was encountered by RCMP-related highway patrol personnel while making his way back towards civilisation. The attending officer described him as confused, agitated and appearing unwell. Although the officer initially thought he looked intoxicated, he specifically noted that he could detect no smell of alcohol. That contemporary observation is significant because it predates the publicity surrounding the case and confirms that Michalak appeared physically and mentally affected soon after the alleged encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library-archives.canada.ca/eng/collection/engage-learn/podcast/Pages/ufo-falcon-lake-incident.aspx?wbdisable=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library-archives.canada.ca">[Library and Archives Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library-archives.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Library and Archives Canada UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library and Archives CanadaUFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake Incident - Part 1May 15, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 15, 2019</span></span></span>

After reaching Winnipeg, Michalak received hospital treatment for [burns]({{ 'burns/' | relative_url }}). Contemporary accounts describe first-degree burns across the upper body together with singed hair and damaged clothing. Later investigators and medical practitioners examined the injuries repeatedly, making the Falcon Lake case unusual among UFO reports because there was objective evidence that the witness had suffered some form of physical injury.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library-archives.canada.ca/eng/collection/engage-learn/podcast/Pages/ufo-falcon-lake-incident.aspx?wbdisable=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library-archives.canada.ca">[Library and Archives Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library-archives.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Library and Archives Canada UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library and Archives CanadaUFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake Incident - Part 1May 15, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 15, 2019</span></span></span>

Michalak also complained of nausea, vomiting, headaches and weakness shortly after the incident. These symptoms became an important part of later retellings, although they were non-specific and could have resulted from numerous medical or environmental causes. Investigators were able to document that he reported these symptoms, but not to determine what produced them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library-archives.canada.ca/eng/collection/engage-learn/podcast/Pages/ufo-falcon-lake-incident.aspx?wbdisable=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library-archives.canada.ca">[Library and Archives Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library-archives.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Library and Archives Canada UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library and Archives CanadaUFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake Incident - Part 1May 15, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 15, 2019</span></span></span>

## Why the burn pattern remains disputed

The feature that attracted the greatest attention was the distinctive pattern on Michalak's abdomen.

According to his account, the burns were produced when a blast of hot gas or exhaust emerged from a grid-like opening in the side of the object as it lifted off. Photographs taken after the incident showed a regular arrangement of burn marks that many later writers described as matching a vent or grille. Because no independent witness saw the alleged blast and no verified craft existed for comparison, however, there is no way to test whether the pattern genuinely corresponded to the source Michalak described.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library-archives.canada.ca/eng/collection/engage-learn/podcast/Pages/ufo-falcon-lake-incident.aspx?wbdisable=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library-archives.canada.ca">[Library and Archives Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library-archives.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Library and Archives Canada UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library and Archives CanadaUFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake Incident - Part 1May 15, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 15, 2019</span></span></span>

The injuries themselves therefore support only a limited conclusion: Michalak was burned. They do not identify the mechanism. Thermal burns, chemical burns and contact with a hot industrial object can all produce patterned injuries under some circumstances. While proponents have argued that the regularity of the marks is difficult to explain conventionally, sceptics have noted that unusual-looking burns are not, by themselves, evidence of an unknown technology. No forensic examination established a unique cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-054.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2 - Canada.ca...</span></span></span>

The same caution applies to his damaged clothing. Burned fabric and melted glove material were consistent with exposure to heat, but they did not reveal what generated that heat. The clothing therefore corroborated injury without confirming the explanation attached to it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library-archives.canada.ca/eng/collection/engage-learn/podcast/Pages/ufo-falcon-lake-incident.aspx?wbdisable=true" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library-archives.canada.ca">[Library and Archives Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library-archives.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Library and Archives Canada UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library and Archives CanadaUFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake Incident - Part 1May 15, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 15, 2019</span></span></span>

## Radiation sickness claims versus the records

One of the most persistent claims about Falcon Lake is that Michalak suffered radiation sickness.

The historical record is considerably more restrained than many popular retellings. Investigators did identify radioactive material associated with parts of the wider investigation, and government agencies examined the possibility that radiation might have been relevant. However, no medical examination established that Michalak had acute radiation syndrome, nor did official investigations conclude that radiation exposure caused either his burns or his illness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-054.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2 - Canada.ca...</span></span></span>

His symptoms—including nausea and weakness—are sometimes presented as proof of radiation exposure, but they are medically non-specific. Such complaints can accompany heat stress, anxiety, dehydration, infection, chemical exposure and numerous other conditions. Without dosimetry, characteristic blood changes or other diagnostic evidence, those symptoms cannot reliably distinguish radiation injury from more ordinary causes. The available records therefore support only the narrower conclusion that Michalak became ill after the event, not that radiation was responsible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-054.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2 - Canada.ca...</span></span></span>

## The later recurring lesions

The case became more complicated when Michalak later reported that similar marks had reappeared on his body.

These recurring lesions have often been cited as evidence that the original injury had unusual long-term effects. Yet the evidential basis is weaker than for the initial burns. The later marks did not arise under the same documented circumstances, and medical opinion was divided over their significance. Some physicians found them difficult to explain, while at least one psychiatric assessment suggested that the later lesions were probably self-inflicted rather than evidence of a continuing physical process. No consensus medical explanation emerged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://podcasts.apple.com/gb/podcast/the-falcon-lake-ufo-incident/id1512013782?i=1000758178804" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: podcasts.apple.com">[Apple Podcasts]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">podcasts.apple.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Podcasts The Falcon Lake UFO Incident–Monster Fuzz – Apple Podcasts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Apple PodcastsThe Falcon Lake UFO Incident–Monster Fuzz – Apple PodcastsMarch 30, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 30, 2026</span></span></span>

## What the injuries actually show

The injuries are both the greatest strength and the greatest limitation of the Falcon Lake case.

They strengthen the case because they demonstrate that something happened to Michalak beyond merely reporting an unusual sighting. Contemporary witnesses, doctors and investigators agreed that he had burns and appeared genuinely unwell. That places Falcon Lake in a different category from reports supported only by testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-053.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 1 - Canada.caMay 15, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 15, 2019</span></span></span>

At the same time, the injuries do not identify their own cause. Neither medical examinations, police investigations nor later reviews established that an unknown aircraft, a secret military vehicle, radiation exposure or any other specific mechanism produced them. The evidence documents the existence of injuries, but it stops short of explaining them.

That unresolved gap is why Michalak's burns remain one of the most debated aspects of Manitoba's best-known UFO case. They are well documented enough to resist easy dismissal, yet insufficiently understood to resolve the central question of what, if anything, caused them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-054.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2 - Canada.ca...</span></span></span>
