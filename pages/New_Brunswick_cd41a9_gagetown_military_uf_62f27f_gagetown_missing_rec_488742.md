## Introduction

The most important point about the reported 1992 sighting at Base [Gagetown]({{ 'gagetown/' | relative_url }}) is that the available records cannot either confirm or disprove it. That is not because military archives are necessarily hiding an answer, but because the claim reached the public many years after the alleged event and the surviving public accounts disagree over basic details such as the date. Once the timing becomes uncertain, it becomes extremely difficult to compare the story with flight activity, training schedules, weather observations or range operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=48271" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 48271December 16, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 16, 2005</span></span></span>

This matters because Gagetown is one of Canada's busiest military training areas. ## The conflict over the sighting date

The largest obstacle to investigating the reported encounter is that the public record does not consistently identify when it happened.

The earliest widely cited public database entry, submitted in 2006, lists the occurrence as approximately **1 June 1992** at about 11:20 pm. The report itself acknowledges that the timing is approximate and was filed roughly fourteen years after the alleged event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=48271" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 48271December 16, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 16, 2005</span></span></span>

Later interviews and television retellings have instead placed the event during **early August 1992**, often specifically around **1 August**, while keeping many of the same narrative details about night guard duty, an ammunition storage area and a large silent object. Those later accounts appear to reflect the witness's subsequent recollection rather than a contemporaneous military document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zoom.iprima.cz/zahady-a-konspirace/ufo-vojenska-zakladna-416814" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zoom.iprima.cz">[Prima Zoom]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zoom.iprima.cz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Prima Zoom UFO nad vojenskou základnou USA: Svědectví vojáků se dokonale shodují</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Prima ZoomUFO nad vojenskou základnou USA: Svědectví vojáků se dokonale shodují - Prima ZoomNovember 6, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 6, 2023</span></span></span>

## Which military and weather records would matter

If the exact date were known, several categories of records could potentially help evaluate the report.

Military documentation could include exercise orders, range schedules, aviation movements, helicopter tasking, visiting-unit timetables and ammunition-storage security arrangements. Gagetown routinely hosted Canadian and allied training, making such records potentially relevant when assessing whether an unusual object could instead have been associated with military activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?IdNumber=133129&amp;app=fonandcol" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca">[recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Combat Training Centre [textual record] Archives / Collections and Fonds</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Combat Training Centre [textual record] Archives / Collections and Fonds</span></span></span>

Meteorological records would also matter. Cloud cover, visibility, wind, humidity, moonlight and atmospheric conditions all influence how distant lights and aircraft appear at night. A bright full moon, for example, can dramatically improve silhouette visibility while simultaneously making distance and size harder to judge. The published witness account itself describes clear skies and a full moon, but without an agreed date investigators cannot confidently compare that memory with archived weather observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=48271" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 48271December 16, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 16, 2005</span></span></span>

Personnel records could theoretically identify who else was assigned nearby, whether additional sentries were present and whether anyone filed an incident report. Yet this too depends on knowing which exercise and which night should be examined.

## Why archived Gagetown records are not a simple answer

It is sometimes assumed that military archives preserve a complete operational history that can easily settle disputed events. In practice, that is rarely the case.

Library and Archives Canada does hold substantial collections of CFB Gagetown records covering operational subjects, exercises, planning and related administrative material. However, these are archival collections rather than a complete, instantly searchable day-by-day operational diary. Many files remain subject to access restrictions, and archival descriptions do not indicate that every routine activity or temporary exercise record has survived.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&amp;idnumber=48110&amp;resource=folderlist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca">[recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gc.ca.</span></span></span>

Even if every relevant file were available, investigators would still need a reliable date before knowing which boxes, exercises or units to examine. A search covering all possible nights across June, July and August 1992 would be far broader and much less likely to produce a definitive answer than a search centred on one confirmed evening.

The same limitation applies to any attempt to locate flight logs or temporary exercise documentation. Records may exist, but matching them to an uncertain event is a different problem from simply proving that records survive.

## What missing records do and do not imply

The absence of a confirming document should not automatically be treated as evidence that the reported sighting never happened.

Many unusual observations—whether eventually explained or not—never generate formal military reports. A soldier who saw something puzzling during an exercise might tell colleagues without creating paperwork, particularly if the object appeared briefly and caused no operational disruption. Likewise, a witness who waited many years before speaking publicly would naturally leave little contemporary documentary evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=48271" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 48271December 16, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 16, 2005</span></span></span>

At the same time, the absence of corroborating records also means the account cannot be independently verified. Without supporting documentation, the case remains primarily a retrospective personal testimony rather than an incident established by multiple independent sources.

This distinction is important. Missing records do **not** prove a cover-up, but neither do they prove that nothing unusual occurred. They simply reduce the amount of evidence available for testing competing explanations.

## What the record gap means for the 1992 claim

For historians and investigators, the Gagetown case illustrates a broader problem with delayed eyewitness reports. The central question is no longer only *what was seen*, but also *whether the surviving records can be matched to the event at all*.

Until a contemporaneous document, additional independent witness statement or precisely dated operational record emerges, the reported 1992 sighting cannot be conclusively compared against Gagetown's military activity. The uncertainty over the date prevents flight logs, exercise schedules, weather data and other operational records from functioning as the decisive evidence that readers might expect.

As a result, the surviving Gagetown archives neither authenticate nor refute the reported encounter. They demonstrate that the base was an exceptionally active military training environment, but they cannot close the case because the key information needed to interrogate those records—the exact timing of the alleged sighting—remains uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&amp;idnumber=48110&amp;resource=folderlist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca">[recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gc.ca.</span></span></span>
