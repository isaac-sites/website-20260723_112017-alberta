## Introduction

The reported glow in the [Ebenezer]({{ 'ebenezer/' | relative_url }}) woods is the single hardest feature of the 22 August 1990 incident to explain. A bright meteor or fireball can account for many of the observations made across Atlantic Canada that evening, but it cannot readily explain a stationary light apparently shining through woodland for roughly two hours after the main object seemed to descend. That discrepancy is why the woodland glow remains the key point of debate within the Ebenezer case. Rather than supporting one extraordinary conclusion, it highlights the gap between what witnesses believed they saw and what can be established from the available evidence.

The surviving accounts show that the prolonged glow was reported by only a small number of observers at a distance, without photographs, recovered material or a [confirmed]({{ 'confirmed/' | relative_url }}) inspection of the light itself. That leaves several plausible [explanations]({{ 'explanations-e9ef8e/' | relative_url }}) in play, none of which can be demonstrated conclusively.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uforum.blogspot.com/2010/11/ebenezers-ice-cream-cone-pei-ufo-of.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uforum.blogspot.com">[uforum.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uforum.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ufology Research: Ebenezer&#x27;s Ice Cream Cone: The PEI UFO of 1990November 16, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 16, 2010</span></span></span>

## How long was the glow reportedly visible?

The best-known reconstruction, based largely on National Research Council file summaries discussed by Canadian UFO researcher Chris Rutkowski, describes witnesses seeing a bright object descend towards woodland near Ebenezer before a luminous area remained visible through the trees until approximately 9.15 pm. The reported duration—around two hours—is central to later discussion because it differs sharply from the brief illumination expected from an ordinary meteor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uforum.blogspot.com/2010/11/ebenezers-ice-cream-cone-pei-ufo-of.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uforum.blogspot.com">[uforum.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uforum.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ufology Research: Ebenezer&#x27;s Ice Cream Cone: The PEI UFO of 1990November 16, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 16, 2010</span></span></span>

Importantly, the reports do not establish that observers were standing beside a glowing object. The light was seen from some distance through trees, making its true position difficult to judge. Human observers are generally poor at estimating the distance of lights at night, especially when there are few visual reference points. A bright light beyond the woodland could easily appear to originate within it.

The reported attendance of RCMP officers adds weight to the claim that something visible was present in the general direction indicated by witnesses. However, no published official record describes officers reaching a glowing object, documenting an impact site or recovering physical evidence from the woods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uforum.blogspot.com/2010/11/ebenezers-ice-cream-cone-pei-ufo-of.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uforum.blogspot.com">[uforum.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uforum.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ufology Research: Ebenezer&#x27;s Ice Cream Cone: The PEI UFO of 1990November 16, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 16, 2010</span></span></span>

## Could a meteor have kept glowing?

This is the weakest explanation for the woodland light itself.

A meteor produces light because its passage through the atmosphere creates intense heating and ionisation. Once it slows or reaches the ground, that brilliant atmospheric glow ends. Fresh meteorites may remain hot internally, but they are not expected to shine like a beacon for hours after landing.

This was recognised by astronomers commenting on the case soon afterwards. Charlottetown Astronomy Club president Clive Perry reportedly regarded the witnesses as sincere while expressing doubt that a meteorite could have remained visibly luminous for such a long period. York University astronomer Paul Delaney likewise suggested that, if the reported duration were accurate, another explanation besides a simple meteorite should be considered. Their comments reflected the reported duration rather than direct examination of any recovered object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/NewsClippings/UFO-Newsclipping-Service/UFO-Newsclipping-Service-1990-12-no-257.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[projectaquarius.mufon.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SERVICEU.F.O. This does not invalidate the meteor interpretation for the earlier fireball seen across Atlantic Canada. Instead, it suggests that the lingering glow, if accurately remembered, was probably a separate phenomenon rather than the meteor itself.

## Possible local lights, fires and reflections

Several ordinary explanations remain possible, although none fits every reported detail perfectly.

**A distant artificial light.** Once witnesses believed they had seen an object descend into nearby woods, an existing light beyond the trees—such as a farmhouse, vehicle, work site or utility installation—could have appeared connected to the earlier event. Night-time perspective frequently compresses distances, making remote lights appear much closer than they really are.

**Vehicle headlights.** Cars moving along roads behind woodland can produce stationary-looking illumination when viewed through gaps between trees. Depending on the road layout, the apparent position of the light may change little even as vehicles move.

**A controlled outdoor fire.** A bonfire or agricultural burn could produce a persistent orange or white glow visible through woodland. However, no contemporary [investigation]({{ 'investigation/' | relative_url }}) identified such a fire as the source.

**Atmospheric reflection or haze.** Moisture, haze or thin cloud can scatter artificial lighting, creating a diffuse glow that appears larger or more mysterious than its true source. Such effects are common when observers are looking towards the horizon after dusk.

None of these possibilities has been confirmed for the Ebenezer site. Equally, none has been ruled out by physical evidence because no documented investigation identified the precise source of the reported light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uforum.blogspot.com/2010/11/ebenezers-ice-cream-cone-pei-ufo-of.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uforum.blogspot.com">[uforum.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uforum.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ufology Research: Ebenezer&#x27;s Ice Cream Cone: The PEI UFO of 1990November 16, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 16, 2010</span></span></span>

## Why the glow remains unresolved

The woodland glow survives as the strongest unresolved element because the available evidence is simultaneously intriguing and limited.

On one hand, the reported duration appears inconsistent with a straightforward meteor impact. On the other, there is no corresponding physical evidence for a landed craft or other extraordinary object. The Charlottetown Astronomy Club searched the suspected area the following morning without finding meteorites, wreckage or other convincing traces. Likewise, no authenticated photographs, instrument [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}) or confirmed recovery reports have emerged in the decades since.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uforum.blogspot.com/2010/11/ebenezers-ice-cream-cone-pei-ufo-of.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uforum.blogspot.com">[uforum.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uforum.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ufology Research: Ebenezer&#x27;s Ice Cream Cone: The PEI UFO of 1990November 16, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 16, 2010</span></span></span>

The later stories describing [military recovery]({{ 'military-claims/' | relative_url }}) operations, helicopters and tarpaulin-covered objects do not resolve the glow itself because they derive largely from retrospective testimony rather than contemporaneous documentation. Even if such later accounts are considered separately, they do not establish what witnesses actually observed shining through the trees on the evening of 22 August 1990.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unexplained-mysteries.com/news/339163/enthusiasts-revisit-scene-of-1990-ufo-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unexplained-mysteries.com">[unexplained-mysteries.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unexplained-mysteries.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">enthusiasts revisit scene of 1990 ufo incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unexplained Mysteries...</span></span></span>

## What the woodland glow means for the Ebenezer case

The two-hour glow is best understood as the point where the evidence becomes most uncertain rather than strongest.

The broader pattern of reports across Atlantic Canada fits well with an exceptionally bright fireball observed over a wide region. The prolonged stationary light reported near Ebenezer does not fit that explanation comfortably, but neither does it establish that an unusual object landed in the woods. Instead, it identifies a narrow gap in the available evidence: witnesses consistently remembered a lingering glow, yet no investigation produced a verified source for it.

For that reason, the woodland glow remains unresolved rather than unexplained. The evidence is sufficient to show why the question persists, but insufficient to distinguish confidently between a misidentified local light, an unusual but ordinary terrestrial source, or some other phenomenon that was never independently documented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uforum.blogspot.com/2010/11/ebenezers-ice-cream-cone-pei-ufo-of.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uforum.blogspot.com">[blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uforum.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ufology Research: Ebenezer&#x27;s Ice Cream Cone: The PEI UFO of 1990November 16, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 16, 2010</span></span></span>
