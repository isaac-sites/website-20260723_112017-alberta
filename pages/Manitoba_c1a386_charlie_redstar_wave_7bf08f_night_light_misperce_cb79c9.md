## Introduction

One reason the [Charlie Redstar wave]({{ 'charlie-redstar/' | relative_url }}) became so compelling is that many witnesses sincerely believed they were watching a nearby object manoeuvring over fields and tree lines. Yet in most cases there were no reliable measurements of distance, altitude or speed. Against the exceptionally dark prairie skies of southern [Manitoba]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-manitobas-ufo/' | relative_url }}), a single bright light could appear much closer than it really was, while subtle changes in brightness, atmospheric conditions and the observer's own visual perception could create a convincing impression of hovering, approaching or changing direction. These effects do not explain every report, but they do explain why many sightings were difficult to interpret and why investigators found it hard to reconstruct exactly what witnesses had seen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/Air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap8_section_1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Chapter 8. Medical Facts for Pilots</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation Administration Chapter 8. Medical Facts for Pilots</span></span></span>

Understanding these visual mechanisms is important because they sit at the heart of the Charlie Redstar story. The central question is often not whether witnesses saw a light—they almost certainly did—but whether the apparent movement belonged to the object itself or arose from the way distant lights are perceived under night-time prairie conditions.

## How darkness removes reliable distance and scale cues

Southern Manitoba's open farmland provides long, unobstructed views, but at night it removes many of the visual references people normally use to judge distance. When a bright point of light is viewed against a featureless sky, the brain has little information from which to estimate whether it is hundreds of metres away or many kilometres distant.

Unlike daytime objects, a distant light offers almost no clues about:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* its true size;
* its distance;
* whether it is moving towards the observer or simply brightening;
* whether it is high above the ground or merely appearing low because it lies near the horizon.

</div>

Without nearby buildings, hills or clearly illuminated terrain for comparison, even experienced observers can overestimate how close a light appears. A bright aircraft landing light, an illuminated tower beacon or a bright astronomical object near the horizon may therefore seem to hover just beyond a line of trees when it is actually much farther away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/19_phak_ch17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">19 phak ch17</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationPHAK Chapter 17May 3, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 3, 2023</span></span></span>

This lack of depth cues is particularly relevant to Charlie Redstar because many observations were made from rural roads, fields and farmyards after dark, where few fixed reference points existed.

## Why haze, trees and dimming can mimic low flight

Many Charlie Redstar descriptions include reports that the light seemed to descend behind trees, emerge again, or move slowly at low altitude. Those impressions can arise from several ordinary visual effects acting together.

Atmospheric haze reduces contrast and scatters light. As a distant light passes through layers of uneven air, its brightness may fluctuate dramatically. Rather than appearing steadily illuminated, it can pulse or fade, creating the impression of controlled movement or changes in altitude. Aviation guidance recognises that haze also distorts perceived distance, making it difficult to judge how far away lights actually are.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim/aim0801.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Chapter 8. Medical Facts for Pilots</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationChapter 8. Medical Facts for PilotsJanuary 8, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 8, 2015</span></span></span>

Tree lines can reinforce the illusion. A distant light partially hidden by branches may appear to weave between them even though only the observer's viewing angle is changing. Likewise, slight changes in terrain or roadside position can make a distant object disappear behind vegetation before reappearing moments later, suggesting deliberate manoeuvring.

Brightness itself also influences perceived distance. A light that suddenly intensifies may seem to be approaching, while one that fades can appear to recede or turn away, even if its position changes very little. On clear prairie nights these changes can be surprisingly convincing because there are so few competing visual references.

## Why stationary lights sometimes appear to move

One of the best-studied night-time perception effects is **autokinesis**. When a person stares at a single bright light against an otherwise dark background for several seconds, the light may appear to drift, zigzag or circle despite remaining stationary.

This illusion has been recognised for decades in aviation because pilots have mistaken stars, planets or distant aircraft lights for moving traffic. It occurs because tiny involuntary eye movements are interpreted by the brain as movement of the light when no surrounding reference points are available. Aviation authorities therefore advise pilots to avoid prolonged fixation on isolated lights and instead scan the wider scene.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faasafety.gov/files/events/SO/SO15/2024/SO15134204/YourSensesInTheShadows.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faasafety.gov">[faasafety.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faasafety.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Your Senses In The Shadows</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Your Senses in the Shadows. Nighttime Visual Illusions and Spatial… &#124; by FAA Safety Briefing Magazine &#124; Jan, 2025 &#124; Medium...</span></span></span>

The effect does **not** produce dramatic high-speed manoeuvres, but it can create convincing impressions of:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* slow sideways drift;
* hovering followed by slight movement;
* gentle climbs or descents;
* hesitation before moving again.

</div>

Those descriptions resemble parts of numerous Charlie Redstar accounts, particularly observations involving a single distant red or orange light watched for extended periods.

## Which reported movements remain difficult to reconstruct

These perceptual effects explain why investigators are cautious about treating estimated speeds, distances or flight paths in the Charlie Redstar wave as precise observations. Most reports lacked photographs with measurable reference points, simultaneous observations from separated locations or radar confirmation. As a result, later researchers often cannot determine whether witnesses were describing one object, several unrelated lights, or different interpretations of similar atmospheric conditions.

That does not mean every reported movement can be dismissed as illusion. Some accounts involved multiple observers viewing the same light simultaneously, while others described changes that are impossible to reconstruct because the necessary observational data were never recorded. In many cases the available evidence is simply insufficient to distinguish confidently between a distant aircraft, an astronomical object affected by atmospheric conditions, an isolated tower light seen under unusual viewing geometry, or something genuinely unidentified.

For that reason, historians of the Manitoba wave increasingly treat apparent manoeuvring as evidence that people perceived unusual movement rather than proof that the light physically executed those manoeuvres. This distinction helps explain why Charlie Redstar remains an important episode in Manitoba's UFO history: not because every movement has been explained or confirmed, but because the combination of dark prairie skies, repeated observation and human visual perception made reliable reconstruction exceptionally difficult.

    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
