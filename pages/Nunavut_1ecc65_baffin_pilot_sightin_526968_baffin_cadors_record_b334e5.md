## Introduction

The strongest piece of evidence in the Baffin Island [pilot sighting]({{ 'pilot-sighting/' | relative_url }}) is not the unidentified light itself but the fact that it entered Canada's official aviation reporting system. The Civil Aviation Daily Occurrence Reporting System (CADORS) confirms that a real aviation occurrence was reported by a commercial flight crew after a 24 November 2018 flight towards the Mary River mine in northern Baffin Island. It provides an authenticated administrative record with a date, location, flight context and reporting chain. What it does **not** do is identify the object or conclude that the crew encountered an extraordinary phenomenon. Contemporary statements from Transport Canada explicitly described the information as preliminary and subject to change, while the occurrence itself was recorded as having no operational impact on the flight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/65674pilots_spot_possible_ufo_above_nunavuts_northern_baffin_island/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nunatsiaq.com">[Nunatsiaq News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nunatsiaq.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Pilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nunatsiaq NewsPilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin IslandNovember 27, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 2018</span></span></span>

## What CADORS recorded about the flight

CADORS is Transport Canada's Civil Aviation Daily Occurrence Reporting System, a database used to record aviation occurrences reported by pilots, airlines, airports and air traffic services. Its purpose is to share operational safety information quickly rather than publish definitive investigation findings.

For the Baffin Island event, the record establishes several important facts:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a Nolinor Aviation Boeing 737-200 crew reported an unusual light while approaching the Mary River mine;
* the occurrence happened on 24 November 2018 at about 8:30 p.m. local time;
* the report was entered into the Canadian civil aviation reporting system;
* the occurrence was categorised under a reporting code that included weather balloons, meteors, rockets and CIRVIS/UFO;
* NORAD was notified through established reporting channels; and
* the flight experienced **"no impact to operations."**<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/65674pilots_spot_possible_ufo_above_nunavuts_northern_baffin_island/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nunatsiaq.com">[Nunatsiaq News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nunatsiaq.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Pilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nunatsiaq NewsPilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin IslandNovember 27, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 2018</span></span></span>

</div>

These points are valuable because they come from an official operational record rather than later retellings. They verify that the event was reported promptly within Canada's aviation system instead of emerging years later through hearsay or folklore.

Just as importantly, the record is brief. It contains no detailed witness interview, no analysis of astronomical conditions, no radar assessment, no reconstruction of the light's movement and no final identification.

## What the CIRVIS and UFO category means

One of the most misunderstood parts of the CADORS entry is its reference to **CIRVIS/UFO**.

CIRVIS stands for **Communications Instructions for Reporting Vital Intelligence Sightings**, a long-standing North American reporting procedure originating in Cold War military reporting guidance. Under these procedures, pilots are expected to report unusual aerial observations that could conceivably have defence or aviation significance. Historically, the reporting categories covered a wide range of observations, including unidentified aircraft, missiles, unusual vessels and unidentified flying objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uforum.blogspot.com/2018/12/nothing-too-baffling-at-baffin-island.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uforum.blogspot.com">[Uforum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uforum.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Uforum Ufology Research: Nothing too baffling at Baffin Island [UPDATED</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ufology Research: Nothing too baffling at Baffin Island [UPDATED December 7, 2018]December 3, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 7, 2018</span></span></span>

Within CADORS, however, **CIRVIS/UFO is simply one occurrence category among several**. The same category also encompasses possible weather balloons, meteors and rockets. It functions as a routing and classification label rather than an assessment that an unidentified object has been confirmed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/65674pilots_spot_possible_ufo_above_nunavuts_northern_baffin_island/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nunatsiaq.com">[Nunatsiaq News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nunatsiaq.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Pilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nunatsiaq NewsPilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin IslandNovember 27, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 2018</span></span></span>

Transport Canada has subsequently clarified this broader usage, noting that "UFO" entries in CADORS may involve drones, balloons, meteors, weather phenomena or birds as well as genuinely unidentified observations. The classification exists to ensure reports are captured, not to imply an exotic explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tc.canada.ca/en/binder/4-high-altitude-object-incidents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tc.canada.ca">[Transport Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tc.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Transport Canada4. ## Why an occurrence report is not a final finding

Readers often assume that an official database entry represents the outcome of an investigation. CADORS works differently.

The system is designed to distribute operational information rapidly. Entries are often created soon after an occurrence is reported so that relevant aviation organisations are aware of potential hazards. They are not equivalent to a completed accident investigation or technical report.

That distinction was made explicitly in the aftermath of the Baffin Island sighting. Transport Canada stated that the information in the occurrence record should be treated as **preliminary, unsubstantiated and subject to change**, and that any follow-up depends on the nature of the reported event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/65674pilots_spot_possible_ufo_above_nunavuts_northern_baffin_island/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nunatsiaq.com">[Nunatsiaq News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nunatsiaq.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Pilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nunatsiaq NewsPilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin IslandNovember 27, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 2018</span></span></span>

As a result, the CADORS record does not answer questions such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* what the light actually was;
* whether radar detected a corresponding object;
* whether weather or astronomical analysis explained it;
* whether investigators interviewed the crew in depth; or
* whether later evidence strengthened or weakened any proposed explanation.

</div>

Those questions require evidence outside the occurrence database.

## What the record really proves—and what it does not

The Baffin Island CADORS entry has genuine evidential value, but its significance is narrower than many online discussions suggest.

It supports several conclusions with confidence:

* the crew genuinely reported an unusual aerial observation;
* the report entered Canada's official civil aviation reporting system;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/aviation-publications/civil-aviation-daily-occurrence-reporting-system-cadors-manual-tp-4044" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tc.canada.ca">[tc.canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tc.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ca Civil Aviation Daily Occurrence Reporting System (CADORS) Manual</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aviation Daily Occurrence Reporting System (CADORS) Manual - TP 4044September 5, 2025 — CIVIL AVIATION DAILY OCCURRENCE REPORTING SYSTEM...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 5, 2025</span></span></span>

It does **not** establish that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the object was an aircraft, spacecraft or physical craft of any particular type;
* the phenomenon displayed extraordinary performance;
* investigators ruled out natural explanations;
* the object remained unidentified after detailed investigation; or

Those are much stronger claims than the official record supports.

For that reason, the CADORS entry is best understood as evidence of a documented pilot report rather than evidence for any specific explanation. Within [Nunavut]({{ 'what-really-lies-behind-nunavuts-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s UFO history, its importance lies in providing an authentic administrative trail for the sighting, not in proving what the pilots ultimately observed.
