## Introduction

The Royal Canadian Mint’s 2023 collector coin transformed the 1970 Duncan hospital UFO story from a largely regional case into a nationally discussed historical curiosity. The glow-in-the-dark silver coin celebrated one of [British Columbia]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-british/' | relative_url }})’s best-known UFO reports, attracting collectors, mainstream media attention and renewed public interest. Ironically, that publicity also prompted local residents to come forward with a detailed alternative explanation: that the mysterious object had been an elaborate homemade hot-air balloon launched as a New Year’s prank. Rather than strengthening the original UFO claim, the coin helped reopen public discussion of the evidence and encouraged fresh testimony that substantially altered how many researchers now assess the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mint.ca/en/shop/coins/2023/1-oz-pure-silver-glow-dark-coin-canadas-unexplained-phenomena-duncan-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mint.ca">[https://www.mint.ca/en-us]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mint.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

## Why the Mint chose the Duncan incident

The Duncan encounter became the sixth release in the Royal Canadian Mint's *Canada's Unexplained Phenomena* series, a collection celebrating famous Canadian mystery stories rather than certifying them as historical fact. The rectangular one-ounce silver coin depicts the nurse's reported view from Cowichan District Hospital, complete with a brightly illuminated saucer and two apparent occupants visible beneath a transparent dome. Under ultraviolet light, additional glowing effects recreate the dramatic lighting described in [witness accounts]({{ 'witness-accounts/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mint.ca/en/shop/coins/2023/1-oz-pure-silver-glow-dark-coin-canadas-unexplained-phenomena-duncan-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mint.ca">[https://www.mint.ca/en-us]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mint.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

The Mint presented the coin as an artistic interpretation of reported eyewitness testimony. Its accompanying description summarised the traditional narrative of the incident and included commentary from UFO researcher Chris Rutkowski describing the case as unexplained, but nowhere suggested that the coin represented an official government finding that an extraterrestrial craft had been observed. Instead, it formed part of a long-running series devoted to notable episodes in Canadian folklore and unexplained history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mint.ca/en/shop/coins/2023/1-oz-pure-silver-glow-dark-coin-canadas-unexplained-phenomena-duncan-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mint.ca">[https://www.mint.ca/en-us]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mint.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

## How publicity brought new witnesses forward

The collector coin generated extensive coverage across British Columbia and beyond during late 2023. That publicity reached people who had been present in Duncan on New Year's Eve 1969–70 and who said they recognised the event immediately—not as a UFO encounter, but as a local prank.

One of the most significant developments came when local resident Dan Hughes described helping to construct and launch a large homemade hot-air balloon. According to his account, the group assembled the craft from lightweight wooden framing, a dry-cleaning bag and candles before releasing it into the early morning sky. Hughes argued that this balloon drifted towards the hospital, matching the timing of the reported sighting. His recollection was supported by other local residents who remembered the prank or aspects of its preparation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vicnews.com/news/witness-to-duncan-ufo-doubts-prank-explanation-offered-in-wake-of-stamp-7116762" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vicnews.com">[Victoria News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vicnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Victoria NewsWitness to Duncan UFO doubts prank explanation offered in wake of stamp - Greater Victoria News...</span></span></span>

The chronology is important. These accounts did not emerge during decades of UFO debate. They appeared only after widespread media attention generated by the Mint's coin prompted [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses/' | relative_url }}) to realise that a local story they had regarded as an old prank was now being celebrated nationally as an unexplained mystery. In that sense, the commemorative coin unintentionally became the catalyst for new historical evidence.

## Why the prank explanation remains debated

The later testimony provides a plausible explanation for the bright airborne object reported outside the hospital, but it does not eliminate every point of disagreement.

Some participants in the original incident continued to reject the prank explanation. Former patient Joan Hieta, who was in the hospital room during the event, maintained in 2023 that she remembered seeing what appeared to be illuminated panels and dark figures inside the object. She argued that what she witnessed seemed much more substantial than a homemade balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vicnews.com/news/witness-to-duncan-ufo-doubts-prank-explanation-offered-in-wake-of-stamp-7116762" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vicnews.com">[Victoria News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vicnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Victoria NewsWitness to Duncan UFO doubts prank explanation offered in wake of stamp - Greater Victoria News...</span></span></span>

Equally, supporters of the prank account acknowledge that memories recalled more than fifty years later inevitably raise questions about precision and detail. There are no surviving contemporary photographs of the balloon launch, and the reconstruction relies primarily on recollections recorded decades after the event. Nevertheless, the account is notable because it offers a coherent mechanism capable of explaining why several people independently observed a brightly lit object near the hospital at the relevant time.

For historians, the emergence of an alternative explanation more than half a century after the event illustrates how public memory can continue to evolve when forgotten participants are prompted to speak.

## Why commemoration is not official confirmation

One of the most common misunderstandings created by the coin is the assumption that government-issued commemorative products validate the events they portray. In reality, the Royal Canadian Mint has long produced collector coins celebrating myths, legends, historical mysteries and unexplained phenomena alongside conventional historical subjects.

The Duncan coin should therefore be understood as recognising the cultural importance of the story within Canadian folklore and British Columbia's UFO history, not as an official endorsement of the original witness interpretation. The Mint's role was to commemorate a famous Canadian mystery through numismatic art rather than to resolve the historical debate over what occurred outside Cowichan District Hospital in January 1970.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mint.ca/en/shop/coins/2023/1-oz-pure-silver-glow-dark-coin-canadas-unexplained-phenomena-duncan-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mint.ca">[https://www.mint.ca/en-us]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mint.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

## What the coin changed in the Duncan case

The Mint's release had two lasting effects on the Duncan encounter.

First, it expanded awareness of the incident far beyond British Columbia, introducing it to collectors and readers who had never encountered the case before. The striking glow-in-the-dark design ensured the story reached audiences well outside the traditional UFO community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mint.ca/en/shop/coins/2023/1-oz-pure-silver-glow-dark-coin-canadas-unexplained-phenomena-duncan-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mint.ca">[https://www.mint.ca/en-us]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mint.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

Second, and more importantly for historical assessment, it reopened the evidential record. Instead of reinforcing the classic close-encounter narrative, the publicity encouraged local residents to provide new testimony suggesting that a New Year's prank may have lain behind one of British Columbia's most famous UFO reports.
