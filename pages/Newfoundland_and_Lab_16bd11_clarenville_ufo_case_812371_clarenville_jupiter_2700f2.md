## Introduction

The leading conventional explanation for the 1978 [Clarenville]({{ 'clarenville/' | relative_url }}) UFO sighting is that witnesses were observing the planet Jupiter under unusually deceptive viewing conditions. That explanation has genuine strengths: a very bright planet seen low over dark water can appear to shimmer, change colour and even seem to hover or move when viewed through turbulent air. However, it does not fully account for every detail later reported by witnesses, particularly descriptions of a structured object or apparent interactions with lights on the ground. The result is a case that sits in an interesting middle ground within [Newfoundland and Labrador]({{ 'newfoundland-and-labrador/' | relative_url }})'s UFO history: one where the best sceptical explanation is plausible, but where some reported features remain disputed rather than cleanly resolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2018/11/ufo-identification-process/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer UFO Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer UFO Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

## Why Jupiter became the main conventional explanation

The Jupiter hypothesis rests on a combination of astronomy and human perception rather than on any single decisive piece of evidence.

Investigators and sceptical commentators have noted that bright planets have repeatedly generated UFO reports around the world. Jupiter is bright enough to dominate the night sky, especially when it is relatively low above the horizon. Under those circumstances it can appear unusually large, intensely luminous and isolated from surrounding stars. People unfamiliar with the night sky often judge such an object to be much closer than it really is.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2018/11/ufo-identification-process/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer UFO Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer UFO Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

The Clarenville reports also describe an object that remained in roughly the same area of the sky for an extended period. A stationary or only slowly changing position is far more consistent with an astronomical object than with an aircraft or an unknown craft travelling through the atmosphere. While Earth's rotation causes Jupiter to move gradually across the sky, that motion is slow enough that casual observers may perceive it as hovering for an hour or more.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.productofnewfoundland.ca/articles/ufos-of-newfoundland" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: productofnewfoundland.ca">[Product of Newfoundland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">productofnewfoundland.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on productofnewfoundland.ca.</span></span></span>

Unlike explanations involving secret aircraft or extraordinary technology, the Jupiter interpretation does not require introducing any unknown phenomenon. It relies on well-documented observational effects that have produced many similar UFO reports elsewhere.

## How atmosphere and dark water can distort a bright planet

The strongest part of the Jupiter explanation is not simply that Jupiter is bright. It is the combination of a bright point of light, atmospheric conditions and human perception.

When a bright planet is viewed through thick layers of atmosphere near the horizon, turbulence bends its light differently from moment to moment. This process, known as scintillation, can make the object flash between red, blue and white, appear to pulse or seem to change shape. Looking through binoculars or a telescope magnifies these distortions instead of removing them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2018/11/ufo-identification-process/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer UFO Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer UFO Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

Viewing across dark water introduces another complication. At night there are few visual reference points for judging distance, size or movement. Without nearby landmarks, observers can struggle to determine whether a light is actually moving or whether small eye movements are creating the illusion of motion.

Psychologists and UFO investigators have also long recognised the autokinetic effect. When someone stares at a bright stationary light against a featureless dark background, tiny involuntary movements of the eyes can make the light appear to drift, wobble or respond to the observer's actions. This illusion becomes more convincing the longer someone watches.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2018/11/ufo-identification-process/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer UFO Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer UFO Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

Taken together, these effects explain several reported characteristics of the Clarenville object:

* its apparent ability to remain almost stationary;
* flashing or changing colours;
* subtle apparent movement;
* an unusually brilliant appearance; and
* a tendency to look different through magnified optics.

## Which witness details remain difficult to reconcile

The Jupiter explanation is persuasive for some aspects of the case, but it is not a perfect fit.

Several witnesses, including RCMP officer James [Blackwood]({{ 'blackwood/' | relative_url }}), later described seeing what appeared to be an oval object with structural features rather than merely a bright point of light. Through a telescope, some accounts mention an elongated or fin-like shape. Atmospheric distortion can stretch and deform bright sources, but whether it could consistently create the same impression for multiple observers remains open to debate because no photographs through the telescope survive for comparison.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.productofnewfoundland.ca/articles/ufos-of-newfoundland" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: productofnewfoundland.ca">[Product of Newfoundland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">productofnewfoundland.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on productofnewfoundland.ca.</span></span></span>

Another frequently repeated claim is that lights on the object appeared to respond after Blackwood activated his police vehicle's emergency lights. This is among the most memorable elements of the story, yet it is also one of the weakest pieces of evidence. Human observers are naturally inclined to connect events occurring close together in time, particularly during an unusual experience. Without independent recordings or repeated demonstrations, coincidence cannot be ruled out.

The reported duration also cuts both ways. A long observation favours a bright astronomical object over a meteor or other transient phenomenon, but it also gave observers ample time to scrutinise the light, discuss what they believed they were seeing and potentially reinforce one another's interpretations.

## Does Jupiter fully explain the Clarenville sighting?

The available evidence supports a cautious answer.

Jupiter provides a credible explanation for the broad characteristics of the event: a bright, apparently stationary light visible for an extended period, showing colour changes and optical distortions under night-time viewing conditions. These are well-understood effects that have contributed to many historical UFO reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2018/11/ufo-identification-process/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer UFO Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer UFO Identification Process &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

At the same time, the explanation does not conclusively account for every reported detail.
