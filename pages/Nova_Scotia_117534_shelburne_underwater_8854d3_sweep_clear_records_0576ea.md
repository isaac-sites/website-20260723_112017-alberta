## Introduction

The most important point about the [Shelburne underwater]({{ 'shelburne-claim/' | relative_url }}) UFO story is that part of it can be independently verified. Naval records confirm that **Sweep Clear V** was a genuine NATO minesweeping exercise conducted off Shelburne, [Nova Scotia]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-nova-scotias/' | relative_url }}), in October 1960, involving Canadian and United States warships. Those records establish that the exercise happened, identify participating vessels, and show that it was a substantial Cold War naval operation. They do **not**, however, document extraordinary underwater craft, alien occupants, or a confirmed military encounter with unidentified objects. That distinction is central to assessing the credibility of the later Shelburne claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2015/mdn-dnd/D12-19-13-3-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.gc.ca">[Publications.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE ROYAL CANADIAN NAVY&#x27;S MAGAZINEAugust 28, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 28, 2015</span></span></span>

## What the Naval Records Confirm About Shelburne

### The ships and exercise documented in 1960

For many years, the alleged underwater encounter near Shelburne was difficult to place historically because [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses-3e07e4/' | relative_url }}) recalled a large naval exercise but could not reliably identify its date or participating ships. ### What the surviving documentation doesand does notshow

Researchers, most notably Chris Styles, have argued that surviving operational records, including material from HMCS *Cape Scott*, indicate an unexpected interruption during the exercise. Styles has also reported finding references that he interprets as evidence of an unusually serious operational alert. Those claims have attracted attention because they appear to anchor part of the later witness testimony in authentic naval paperwork.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.popularmechanics.com/science/a68066027/ufo-crash-defcon-mystery-underwater/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: popularmechanics.com">[Popular Mechanics]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">popularmechanics.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo crash defcon mystery underwater</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Although the Coast Guard found only yellow foam at the scene, diver investigations yielded no physical evidence. However, an important distinction remains.

The publicly discussed records confirm:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the existence of Sweep Clear V;
* the approximate timing of the exercise;
* the participating naval forces;
* normal military operations associated with minesweeping training.

</div>

They do **not** publicly describe:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* unidentified underwater vehicles;
* divers observing non-human craft;
* recovery of exotic materials;
* photographs or sonar images of extraordinary objects;
* official conclusions that anything non-conventional was encountered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2015/mdn-dnd/D12-19-13-3-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.gc.ca">[Publications.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE ROYAL CANADIAN NAVY&#x27;S MAGAZINEAugust 28, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 28, 2015</span></span></span>

In other words, the documentary record verifies the military context without independently verifying the extraordinary claims later attached to it.

## What Later Divers Said They Encountered

The dramatic elements of the Shelburne story come primarily from retrospective testimony collected decades after the exercise.

According to Chris Styles' interviews, several former military divers described descending during Sweep Clear V and allegedly finding two unusual craft resting on the seabed, one apparently damaged while another appeared to be assisting it. They further claimed they were ordered back to the surface and instructed not to discuss what they had seen. These recollections became the foundation of the modern Shelburne narrative rather than contemporary naval reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.popularmechanics.com/science/a68066027/ufo-crash-defcon-mystery-underwater/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: popularmechanics.com">[Popular Mechanics]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">popularmechanics.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo crash defcon mystery underwater</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Although the Coast Guard found only yellow foam at the scene, diver investigations yielded no physical evidence. The passage of time matters when evaluating such testimony. Human memory can preserve vivid experiences while simultaneously losing precise dates, locations and sequence of events. Indeed, one of the major developments in Styles' investigation was his conclusion that the witnesses had remembered the exercise itself but had associated it with the wrong year. ## Why Confirming the Exercise Does Not Confirm the Craft

This distinction illustrates an important principle in historical UFO research.

Finding authentic military records that verify an exercise is not the same as finding military records that verify an extraordinary encounter during that exercise.

In the Shelburne case, two separate propositions must be evaluated independently:

Confirmed by documentary evidenceNot independently confirmed by documentary evidenceSweep Clear V occurred in October 1960.Two unidentified underwater craft were present.Canadian and U.S. naval forces participated.Divers encountered occupied vehicles.Minesweeping operations took place near Shelburne.Military personnel were officially ordered to conceal contact with non-human technology.Naval vessels and divers were operating in the area.The exercise was interrupted because of extraterrestrial or otherwise extraordinary objects.

This separation prevents a common logical error: assuming that because the background setting is genuine, every later claim attached to that setting is equally well established.

The Shelburne case therefore occupies an unusual position within Nova Scotia's UFO history. Unlike many stories that lack any identifiable historical framework, its military setting is well documented. Yet the central extraordinary claims remain dependent largely on witness recollections rather than contemporaneous official records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2015/mdn-dnd/D12-19-13-3-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.gc.ca">[Publications.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE ROYAL CANADIAN NAVY&#x27;S MAGAZINEAugust 28, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 28, 2015</span></span></span>

## What This Means for Assessing the Shelburne Story

The archival evidence strengthens one part of the narrative while leaving another unresolved.

The existence of Sweep Clear V removes any suggestion that the military exercise itself was invented. It confirms that a substantial NATO operation involving minesweeping ships, divers and support vessels really occurred off Shelburne in October 1960. That makes the setting historically credible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2015/mdn-dnd/D12-19-13-3-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.gc.ca">[Publications.gc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.gc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE ROYAL CANADIAN NAVY&#x27;S MAGAZINEAugust 28, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 28, 2015</span></span></span>
