## Introduction

Charlie Redstar was the popular name given to recurring red and orange lights reported over southern [Manitoba]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-manitobas-ufo/' | relative_url }}) during 1975 and 1976, particularly around Carman, Sperling, Haywood, Roland and the surrounding Pembina Valley. The wave became remarkable not because of one decisive encounter, but because groups of residents repeatedly gathered to watch the lights, sometimes on successive nights. Witnesses described pulsation, hovering, low-level flight and sudden changes of direction.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Manitoba_c1a386_charlie_redstar_wave_7bf08f-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Charlie Redstar" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The central difficulty is that “Charlie” was never securely established as one object. Reports varied greatly in colour, shape, distance and behaviour, while surviving photographs and official files did not provide dependable measurements of range, speed or size. Some sightings may have involved aircraft, stars, planets, meteors, tower lights or unusual atmospheric viewing conditions. A smaller residue remains hard to reconstruct, but “unexplained” here chiefly means that the necessary evidence was never collected.

## Where and when the lights appeared

The episode’s best-known starting point came near Carman, roughly 80 kilometres south-west of Winnipeg. On 10 April 1975, aircraft builder and pilot Robert “Bob” Diemert and his wife Elaine reportedly saw a large pulsating red light approaching their farm and private airfield. Later retellings describe it as moving slowly above the trees before turning north. An RCMP record confirms that Diemert reported a UFO at his Carman airfield that evening, although the surviving administrative record does not independently establish the object’s shape, altitude or speed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mbnews101.ca/carman-dufferin-standard/fifty-years-have-passed-since-charlie-red-star-phenomenon-in-1975/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mbnews101.ca">[mbnews101.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mbnews101.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">fifty years have passed since charlie red star phenomenon in 1975</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manitoba News 101...</span></span></span>

Further reports followed during April and early May. By 7 May, witnesses were regularly arriving near the Diemert property to watch the sky. The Carman area developed favourite observation points, with lines of cars gathering along gravel roads. Local businesses adopted the story, advertisements joked about shopping where Charlie shopped, and national reporters visited the district. The result was an unusual mixture of repeated observation, community entertainment and media-driven expectation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mbnews101.ca/carman-dufferin-standard/fifty-years-have-passed-since-charlie-red-star-phenomenon-in-1975/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mbnews101.ca">[mbnews101.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mbnews101.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">fifty years have passed since charlie red star phenomenon in 1975</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manitoba News 101...</span></span></span>

The reports did not remain confined to Carman. A later Manitoba UFO catalogue, assembled from government files, newspapers and private investigators’ records, lists numerous 1975 sightings around Sperling, Haywood, Roland, Miami, Elm Creek, St Claude, Portage la Prairie and Winnipeg. The catalogue also records a new concentration around Carman, Sperling and Brunkhild in March and April 1976, followed by intermittent reports through that summer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/c23c8b29-268f-4742-a45e-2dba156b0e52/downloads/bd218863-4433-42e9-a2a5-e727d001770f/Manitoba%20UFO%20Survey%20Data%20Table.pdf?ver=1776956964063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: img1.wsimg.com">[Wsimg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">img1.wsimg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manitoba UFO Survey Data Table</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MB all 2015 edited June 8.xlsx...</span></span></span>

That catalogue is useful for showing the scale and geographical spread of the wave, but its entries are not equivalent to independently verified incidents. They combine reports of very different quality: some came through the National Research Council, the Department of National Defence or the RCMP; others came from newspapers, civilian organisations or Grant Cameron, a local investigator and frequent observer. Several entries contain only brief summaries written long after the event, and some lack exact dates or times.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/c23c8b29-268f-4742-a45e-2dba156b0e52/downloads/bd218863-4433-42e9-a2a5-e727d001770f/Manitoba%20UFO%20Survey%20Data%20Table.pdf?ver=1776956964063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: img1.wsimg.com">[Wsimg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">img1.wsimg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manitoba UFO Survey Data Table</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MB all 2015 edited June 8.xlsx...</span></span></span>

The activity was therefore both concentrated and diffuse. Carman supplied the wave’s name, social centre and most recognisable recurring light. Southern Manitoba more broadly supplied a much less orderly collection of red points, white lights, supposed discs, fireballs and unusual formations. Treating every report as an appearance by the same object makes the story simpler, but less accurate.

## What witnesses said the lights did

The most consistent description was of a red or orange light that brightened and dimmed rhythmically. Some witnesses compared the pulsation to a heartbeat. Others said the light travelled close to the horizon, appeared to pass behind trees or grain elevators, or moved near communications towers before changing direction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/c23c8b29-268f-4742-a45e-2dba156b0e52/downloads/bd218863-4433-42e9-a2a5-e727d001770f/Manitoba%20UFO%20Survey%20Data%20Table.pdf?ver=1776956964063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: img1.wsimg.com">[Wsimg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">img1.wsimg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manitoba UFO Survey Data Table</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MB all 2015 edited June 8.xlsx...</span></span></span>

Group sightings are one reason Charlie Redstar remains more interesting than an isolated light report. People deliberately went out together, and some observations involved several cars or groups at the same location. The catalogue describes five people watching a red light near Carman on 12 May 1975, several witnesses seeing flashes and movement on 31 May, and four people in separate cars repeatedly observing a light near Carman on 29 March 1976.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/c23c8b29-268f-4742-a45e-2dba156b0e52/downloads/bd218863-4433-42e9-a2a5-e727d001770f/Manitoba%20UFO%20Survey%20Data%20Table.pdf?ver=1776956964063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: img1.wsimg.com">[Wsimg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">img1.wsimg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manitoba UFO Survey Data Table</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MB all 2015 edited June 8.xlsx...</span></span></span>

Yet the testimony did not describe a stable, repeatable object. Reports attributed several very different forms and actions to “Charlie” or to associated lights:

* a simple red ball or point source;
* a light with a white centre or coloured fringe;
* a disc, dome or bell shape;
* a large wheel-like form with lights around its edge;
* multiple smaller lights separating from a larger one;
* apparent hovering, zigzagging, looping or sudden acceleration;
* movement along roads, power lines or tower horizons.

The diversity matters. A genuinely recurring physical craft might be expected to show some consistent structure, flight pattern or measurable signature. Instead, the Charlie Redstar label gradually absorbed almost any striking nocturnal light seen during the wave. The catalogue itself sometimes distinguishes probable aircraft, satellites, meteors or other conventional possibilities, while leaving other entries unresolved or insufficiently described.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/c23c8b29-268f-4742-a45e-2dba156b0e52/downloads/bd218863-4433-42e9-a2a5-e727d001770f/Manitoba%20UFO%20Survey%20Data%20Table.pdf?ver=1776956964063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: img1.wsimg.com">[Wsimg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">img1.wsimg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manitoba UFO Survey Data Table</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MB all 2015 edited June 8.xlsx...</span></span></span>

Descriptions of low flight also require caution. At night, a bright point against a dark sky provides almost no natural information about its distance. Without a known background, two observers can agree that a light moved while being badly mistaken about whether it was hundreds of metres away or many kilometres distant. A light passing “behind” trees may indeed be nearby, but it may also simply have dimmed, entered haze or been temporarily concealed along the observer’s line of sight.

The wave nevertheless contained some reports that were harder to reduce to a distant star. Witnesses sometimes described lights crossing broad sections of sky, apparently approaching and receding, or changing course. A few accounts included noise, illuminated ground or reactions from animals. Those details raise the evidential interest, but most survive only as compressed summaries rather than complete statements with maps, weather data, independent bearings and properly timed observations.

## How the wave was recorded

Charlie Redstar left more documentation than many local UFO stories, but less reliable documentation than its fame might suggest. The RCMP received reports, and a number of Manitoba sightings entered National Research Council or defence files. Canada’s federal UFO-record system functioned mainly as a collection and referral process: reports could be passed among police, aviation, military and scientific bodies without implying that any agency had confirmed an exotic object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/canada/Canada%20-%20FOIA%20Part%2008%20-%20Pages%202101-2400.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[documents.theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Canada FOIA Part 08 Pages</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Canada FOIA Part 08 Pages</span></span></span>

Newspapers were central to the wave. They publicised new sightings, attracted investigators and encouraged more residents to look upwards. Contemporary titles cited in later historical accounts include reports in the *Winnipeg Free Press*, *Brandon Sun* and several western Canadian newspapers. Some coverage offered sceptical interpretations, including the possibility that widely reported lights elsewhere in the 1975 Canadian wave were Jupiter. Other articles emphasised mystery, witness credibility or the social excitement surrounding UFO watching.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://firesidecanada.ca/charlie-redstar-manitobas-friendly-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: firesidecanada.ca">[firesidecanada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">firesidecanada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">charlie redstar manitobas friendly ufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Charlie Redstar: Manitoba&#x27;s Friendly UFO16 Mar 2023 — “UFOs seem common the Westman area.” The Brandon Sun, 2 December 1975, p 3...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1975</span></span></span>

This attention produced both useful and contaminating effects. It created a rough public chronology and brought witnesses forward quickly. At the same time, it taught observers what Charlie was supposed to look like and where it was expected to appear. Once nightly sky-watching parties had formed, ordinary lights that would previously have attracted little notice were examined intensely and interpreted through an established story.

Grant Cameron became the wave’s most influential chronicler. He said he saw the lights repeatedly, organised viewing trips and interviewed large numbers of witnesses. His later book preserved details that might otherwise have disappeared. It is therefore an important historical source, but also an involved witness-investigator account rather than a neutral scientific study. The book’s claim that Charlie Redstar represented one of North America’s largest UFO episodes rests heavily on accumulated testimony and Cameron’s reconstruction of events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://epl.bibliocommons.com/v2/record/S980C3028431" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: epl.bibliocommons.com">[Edmonton Public Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">epl.bibliocommons.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Edmonton Public Library Charlie Red Star &#124; Edmonton Public Library</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Edmonton Public LibraryCharlie Red Star &#124; Edmonton Public Library - BiblioCommons...</span></span></span>

A later Manitoba UFO survey brought together entries from Cameron, federal files, civilian groups and media sources. It shows an unmistakable reporting spike during 1975–76, but it also reveals how uneven the underlying material is. Some records list several witnesses and long durations; others record little more than a place, colour and short description. Its classifications range from probable conventional objects to unresolved cases, underscoring that the “wave” was a case family rather than a single evidentially uniform incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/c23c8b29-268f-4742-a45e-2dba156b0e52/downloads/bd218863-4433-42e9-a2a5-e727d001770f/Manitoba%20UFO%20Survey%20Data%20Table.pdf?ver=1776956964063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: img1.wsimg.com">[Wsimg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">img1.wsimg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manitoba UFO Survey Data Table</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MB all 2015 edited June 8.xlsx...</span></span></span>

## Why ordinary lights could look extraordinary

No single conventional explanation fits every Charlie Redstar report. There are, however, several mechanisms capable of producing much of the pattern without requiring one unknown craft to return night after night.

**Aircraft lights** are an obvious candidate. Southern Manitoba lay beneath active civil and private aviation routes, while the Carman focus began at an airfield owned by an experienced aircraft builder. An approaching aircraft can appear stationary for minutes when travelling almost directly towards an observer. It may then seem to turn abruptly as its angle changes, while its red, green, white or rotating beacon lights brighten and fade through haze.

Aircraft do not explain every account, particularly those describing extremely low silent objects or unusual structures. But those estimates were generally visual rather than instrumented. A distant aircraft viewed head-on can appear as one red or white point; after turning, it can suddenly reveal additional lights and seem to change shape.

**Stars and planets** can also imitate a hovering, pulsating object. Near the horizon, atmospheric turbulence continually bends and separates their light, producing rapid changes of colour and brightness. The autokinetic effect—a well-known illusion in which a stationary light appears to drift when viewed against darkness—can make a planet or tower lamp seem to move, circle or react to an observer. Contemporary Canadian reporting during the wider 1975 wave specifically identified Jupiter as an explanation for some heavily publicised sightings, although that does not establish that Jupiter caused every Carman report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://firesidecanada.ca/charlie-redstar-manitobas-friendly-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: firesidecanada.ca">[firesidecanada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">firesidecanada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">charlie redstar manitobas friendly ufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Charlie Redstar: Manitoba&#x27;s Friendly UFO16 Mar 2023 — “UFOs seem common the Westman area.” The Brandon Sun, 2 December 1975, p 3...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1975</span></span></span>

**Communications towers and distant ground lights** deserve particular attention because many observations were made towards known towers or low over the horizon. Refraction, haze and temperature layers over flat prairie terrain can make distant lights appear enlarged, displaced or intermittently hidden. A moving observer in a car may also experience parallax: a remote fixed light seems to follow the vehicle while nearer trees and buildings move across the field of view.

**Meteors, satellites and re-entering debris** better fit shorter events described as streaks, fireballs or lights crossing a large arc of sky. The Manitoba catalogue itself marks several reports as probable meteors, satellites or aircraft. These explanations cannot account for objects said to remain visible for hours, but they show that unlike phenomena were being grouped into the same wave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/c23c8b29-268f-4742-a45e-2dba156b0e52/downloads/bd218863-4433-42e9-a2a5-e727d001770f/Manitoba%20UFO%20Survey%20Data%20Table.pdf?ver=1776956964063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: img1.wsimg.com">[Wsimg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">img1.wsimg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manitoba UFO Survey Data Table</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MB all 2015 edited June 8.xlsx...</span></span></span>

**Expectation and social reinforcement** probably enlarged the number and variety of reports. This does not mean witnesses were dishonest. Once Charlie Redstar became a local celebrity, more people watched the night sky for longer periods, often from the same roads and fields. They encountered objects and visual effects that most people rarely study. Reports were discussed between observers, repeated in newspapers and folded back into later expectations. The traffic jams and organised viewing parties around Carman provide unusually clear evidence that observation itself had become a community event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mbnews101.ca/carman-dufferin-standard/fifty-years-have-passed-since-charlie-red-star-phenomenon-in-1975/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mbnews101.ca">[mbnews101.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mbnews101.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">fifty years have passed since charlie red star phenomenon in 1975</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manitoba News 101...</span></span></span>

## Why the wave remains difficult to assess

The strongest evidence for Charlie Redstar is the volume of sincere testimony. Numerous people said they saw unusual lights, sometimes together and sometimes repeatedly. Reports were made while the wave was active, not invented decades later, and some entered police or federal channels. It is therefore reasonable to conclude that southern Manitoba experienced a genuine wave of unusual-light reports.

That conclusion is more limited than it first appears. The reports do not demonstrate that all witnesses observed the same object, or that any object performed the extreme manoeuvres later attributed to it. The surviving record lacks the types of data needed to answer those questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no securely calibrated series of photographs showing a recognisable object;
* no published triangulation from observers at separated locations;
* no dependable radar track tied to a specific Charlie sighting;
* no recovered material with a documented chain of custody;
* no complete matching of reports against aircraft movements, astronomical positions and weather conditions;
* no instrumented measurements of altitude, distance, acceleration or luminosity.

</div>

Photographs were reportedly taken during the wave, including several exposures in July 1975, but the catalogue describes them mainly as changing or enlarged lights. Without the camera settings, original negatives, fixed reference points and independent positional data, an image of a bright blob cannot distinguish a nearby object from an out-of-focus star, aircraft light or camera movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/c23c8b29-268f-4742-a45e-2dba156b0e52/downloads/bd218863-4433-42e9-a2a5-e727d001770f/Manitoba%20UFO%20Survey%20Data%20Table.pdf?ver=1776956964063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: img1.wsimg.com">[Wsimg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">img1.wsimg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manitoba UFO Survey Data Table</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MB all 2015 edited June 8.xlsx...</span></span></span>

The passage of time compounds the problem. Many detailed accounts now circulate through retrospective books, podcasts and popular websites, often depending on the same small set of witness narratives. Repetition can make a claim appear independently corroborated even when later versions trace back to one interview or one investigator’s notes.

Claims linking the wave to American missile or nuclear activity are especially weak. Cameron reported rumours of testing south of the international border, and publishers have repeated a possible connection to United States defence operations. No publicly documented sequence has demonstrated that particular tests coincided with the Manitoba lights, caused them or attracted them. The suggestion remains speculative rather than an established military explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://epl.bibliocommons.com/v2/record/S980C3028431" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: epl.bibliocommons.com">[Edmonton Public Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">epl.bibliocommons.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Edmonton Public Library Charlie Red Star &#124; Edmonton Public Library</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Edmonton Public LibraryCharlie Red Star &#124; Edmonton Public Library - BiblioCommons...</span></span></span>

## What Charlie Redstar means in Manitoba’s UFO history

Charlie Redstar matters because it illustrates a different kind of UFO case from Manitoba’s [Falcon Lake]({{ 'falcon-lake/' | relative_url }}) incident. Falcon Lake centres on one witness, an alleged close encounter and contested physical traces. Charlie Redstar centres on repetition, crowds and the gradual construction of a shared identity around ambiguous lights.

Its historical importance is secure. The wave produced reports across southern Manitoba, attracted police and media attention, encouraged local investigation and became part of Carman’s civic folklore. Fifty years later, the town continued to mark the anniversary through community events, showing that Charlie has endured as local cultural history even though its physical cause remains unknown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mbnews101.ca/carman-dufferin-standard/fifty-years-have-passed-since-charlie-red-star-phenomenon-in-1975/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mbnews101.ca">[mbnews101.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mbnews101.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">fifty years have passed since charlie red star phenomenon in 1975</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manitoba News 101...</span></span></span>

Its scientific status is much weaker. The record supports a substantial reporting wave, not a verified recurring craft. Some observations were probably ordinary astronomical, aviation or atmospheric phenomena. Others are too poorly documented to classify. A limited number contain features that resist easy explanation in their surviving form, but the absence of essential measurements prevents them from becoming strong evidence for an extraordinary mechanism.

The most defensible assessment is therefore neither that Charlie Redstar was “debunked” as one known object nor that an unknown vehicle repeatedly toured the Manitoba prairie. The name covered a mixed population of sightings, shaped by real observations, uncertain perception, intense media attention and inadequate instrumentation. What remains unresolved is not necessarily what flew over Carman, but whether the most unusual reports recorded something genuinely anomalous or merely lost the information that would once have explained them.
