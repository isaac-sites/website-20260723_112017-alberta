## Introduction

The [Heath Steele Mine]({{ 'mine-explanations/' | relative_url }}) case is often presented as one of [New Brunswick]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-new-brunswicks/' | relative_url }})’s most dramatic UFO encounters: several people reportedly saw an unusual object near the remote mining complex between Bathurst and Miramichi in late October 1989. Later publicity went much further, describing a landed extraterrestrial spacecraft and even a “close encounter of the third kind”. The surviving public record does not justify that level of certainty.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/New_Brunswick_cd41a9_heath_steele_ufo_cla_9aa38a-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Heath Steele" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

What can reasonably be said is narrower. A local multi-witness story appears to have circulated around a real industrial site, and it has remained part of northern New Brunswick folklore for more than three decades. However, the original witness statements, exact observation data, photographs, police paperwork and contemporary technical checks are not readily available in a verifiable public archive. Most accessible references are retrospective broadcasts, commemorative events and material promoting investigator Paul Bédard’s later book. The case may preserve a sincere unexplained observation, but later retellings have clearly outgrown the evidence that independent readers can inspect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://omny.fm/shows/bounce-1049/this-day-in-bathurst-history-ufo-sighting-in-1989" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: omny.fm">[omny.fm]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">omny.fm</span><span class="citation-popover-title">this day in bathurst history ufo sighting in 1989</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Omny StudioThis Day In Bathurst History: UFO Sighting In 1989 - BOUNCE 104.9 -Bathurst - Omny.fm31 years ago today, there were several w...</span></span></span>

## The reported 1989 encounter

The date most consistently implied by later anniversary notices is 30 October 1989. In 2020, Bathurst radio station CKBC marked the event’s thirty-first anniversary by saying that several witnesses had seen a UFO outside Heath Steele Mine. Bathurst library publicity the following year described 30 October as the thirty-second anniversary of the alleged encounter. These notices support the existence of a locally remembered event on or around that date, although they do not themselves provide contemporary documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://omny.fm/shows/bounce-1049/this-day-in-bathurst-history-ufo-sighting-in-1989" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: omny.fm">[omny.fm]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">omny.fm</span><span class="citation-popover-title">this day in bathurst history ufo sighting in 1989</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Omny StudioThis Day In Bathurst History: UFO Sighting In 1989 - BOUNCE 104.9 -Bathurst - Omny.fm31 years ago today, there were several w...</span></span></span>

Heath Steele was an isolated but active copper, lead and zinc operation in north-central New Brunswick, roughly 50 kilometres north-west of Miramichi and about 65 kilometres south-west of Bathurst. It was reached by Route 430 and had its own processing plant, heavy machinery and railway infrastructure. The mine had reopened during 1989 after years of inactivity, making the site an operating industrial complex rather than an empty wilderness location when the alleged sighting occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1136861/000113717107000318/techreport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[sec.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1989 &#124; 473688 &#124; BM&amp;S Corporation Ltd.... The Heath Steele property is situated approximately 65 kilometres southwest of the City of Bath...</span></span></span>

That setting matters. A group of workers or travellers near the mine would have had a relatively dark horizon away from the larger towns, potentially making an unfamiliar light conspicuous. At the same time, the complex contained powerful fixed and moving lights, vehicles, plant equipment and railway activity capable of producing misleading impressions at night. A credible reconstruction would therefore need to establish where each witness stood, which direction they faced, how long the event lasted and whether the object was seen against the sky, above the ground or among mine structures.

Those basic details are difficult to recover from accessible sources. The radio description confirms only “several witnesses” and a UFO outside the mine. It does not publish their names, separate their accounts or reproduce a report made immediately after the event. Nor does it establish whether all witnesses independently described the same shape, distance, motion and duration before discussing the sighting with one another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://omny.fm/shows/bounce-1049/this-day-in-bathurst-history-ufo-sighting-in-1989" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: omny.fm">[Omny Studio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">omny.fm</span><span class="citation-popover-title">this day in bathurst history ufo sighting in 1989</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Omny StudioThis Day In Bathurst History: UFO Sighting In 1989 - BOUNCE 104.9 -Bathurst - Omny.fm31 years ago today, there were several w...</span></span></span>

This does not prove that the story was invented. Multiple people can sincerely observe something they cannot identify. It does mean that “multi-witness” should not automatically be treated as “independently corroborated”. Witness agreement is strongest when statements are recorded separately and promptly; it becomes harder to assess after years of conversation, shared memories and repeated storytelling.

## How later accounts expanded the claim

The most substantial later interpretation is associated with Paul Bédard, who promoted a book in French and English under titles describing the event as the landing of an extraterrestrial spacecraft at Heath Steele Mine. Publicity for appearances in the Bathurst area framed the story not merely as an unidentified sighting but as a historic close encounter. One library notice explicitly called it a “close encounter of the third kind”, a term normally implying the reported presence of occupants or beings as well as a craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/100064583354545/posts/visite-de-lauteur-paul-b%C3%A9dardcomm%C3%A9morez-le-36e-anniversaire-dune-rencontre-de-tr/1229944389168328/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visite de l&#x27;auteur Paul Bédard Commémorez le 36e...... landing of an extraterrestrial spaceship the historic case at the Heath S...</span></span></span>

That represents a major evidential escalation. There is a large difference between saying that several people saw an unexplained object and asserting that an extraterrestrial vehicle landed. The second claim requires evidence for at least four separate propositions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* that a structured object was present rather than a distant light or visual effect;
* that it reached or rested on the ground;
* that the witnesses were close enough to judge its size and behaviour reliably;
* and that ordinary aircraft, industrial activity, astronomy and atmospheric effects were excluded.

</div>

Publicly accessible summaries do not show how those steps were established. They offer conclusions and commemorative language but not the underlying case file needed to test them. No searchable primary record has emerged showing contemporaneous photographs, measured landing traces, instrument readings, radar data or material recovered from the site.

The timing of the investigation is also important. The incident was publicised widely decades after 1989, and the strongest claims appear chiefly in later book-related material. Retrospective testimony can still contain valuable information, particularly when several witnesses remember a striking event. Yet memory is reconstructive: later interviews may preserve a genuine core while adding confidence, detail or interpretation that was absent from the first account. Without the earliest statements, readers cannot tell which elements belong to 1989 and which developed afterwards.

The available promotional language further blurs investigation and advocacy. Calling the case an extraterrestrial landing before independently verifiable evidence is presented effectively assumes the conclusion that the investigation should be testing. That does not make every witness statement false, but it weakens the neutrality of the later presentation.

## What documentation is missing

The Heath Steele claim falls outside the main period covered by Canada’s readily searchable historical federal UFO collection. Library and Archives Canada says its digitised holdings were accumulated from the Department of National Defence, Department of Transport, National Research Council and Royal Canadian Mounted Police between 1947 and the early 1980s. The collection contains about 9,500 digitised documents, but it cannot be expected to contain a complete record of an event reported in 1989.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/science-technology/ufos.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">s UFOs: The search for the unknown</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">s UFOs: The search for the unknown</span></span></span>

Canada also lacked a single, continuous public UFO-investigation programme that automatically created a detailed federal file for every civilian sighting. The federal Sky Canada review found that reporting practices have been fragmented across aviation, police, defence and civilian organisations. Historical records are therefore uneven, and an absence from the principal archive is not proof that no local report was ever made.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[Innovation Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on canada.ca.</span></span></span>

Even so, several records would normally be expected if the event had been investigated as a physical landing:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* original signed or recorded witness statements;
* a map marking observation and alleged landing positions;
* photographs of the site taken soon afterwards;
* RCMP occurrence information, if police were contacted;
* mine security or shift logs;
* aircraft and helicopter checks;
* weather and astronomical data for the stated time;
* and laboratory results for any alleged ground or vegetation effects.

</div>

None of these has been made readily available through the sources that currently sustain the story online. That gap is the case’s central weakness. It prevents meaningful comparison between witnesses and makes ordinary explanations much harder to test nearly four decades later.

The contrast with better-documented Canadian cases is instructive. A disputed UFO incident does not become convincing merely because an official file exists, but a contemporary paper trail at least fixes what was claimed, by whom and when. Heath Steele’s public profile rests largely on later retelling rather than an accessible 1989 record.

## Industrial and aviation explanations that need testing

No single conventional explanation can be responsibly declared proven from the available information. The necessary observation details are missing. Several possibilities nevertheless deserve testing before an extraordinary conclusion is considered.

**Mine and mill lighting.** Heath Steele returned to production in 1989, with a large milling operation processing ore from several zones. An operating mine uses floodlights, vehicle lamps, illuminated buildings and mobile equipment. In darkness, especially when distance cues are poor, separate lights can appear to form a single structured object. Reflections from low cloud, mist, dust or moisture may also make beams seem suspended above the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onemine.org/search?Organization=CIM&amp;SortBy=Relevance&amp;keywords=tailings&amp;page=23&amp;searchfield=all" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onemine.org">[onemine.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onemine.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on onemine.org.</span></span></span>

**Heavy vehicles and railway movements.** The mine was connected to the Canadian National network by a purpose-built railway branch and handled mineral concentrates and supplies. Locomotives, maintenance vehicles or equipment moving along elevated or partly obscured ground could create apparently hovering or shifting lights when the body of the vehicle was hidden. This possibility would depend on the witnesses’ exact position and whether the reported object was visually separated from the mine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://collection.ingenium.ca/en/id/53347-8/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: collection.ingenium.ca">[collection.ingenium.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">collection.ingenium.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ingenium.ca.</span></span></span>

**Aircraft or helicopters.** Heath Steele lay between the Bathurst and Miramichi areas, with aviation facilities in the wider region and a provincial airstrip near the Sevogle River. An aircraft approaching nearly head-on can appear stationary for several minutes because its angular position changes very little; when it turns, it may then seem to accelerate abruptly. Helicopters associated with forestry, surveying, emergency work or mining would also need to be excluded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1136861/000113717107000318/techreport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[sec.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1989 &#124; 473688 &#124; BM&amp;S Corporation Ltd.... The Heath Steele property is situated approximately 65 kilometres southwest of the City of Bath...</span></span></span>

**Astronomical objects.** Bright planets and stars frequently generate UFO reports when viewed close to the horizon, where atmospheric distortion can produce apparent colour changes and movement. This becomes less persuasive if witnesses clearly observed a nearby solid body crossing in front of known terrain, but the accessible summaries do not provide enough geometry to decide that point.

**Atmospheric and perceptual effects.** Temperature inversions can distort distant lights, while haze or low cloud may enlarge and diffuse them. Dark rural environments also remove normal size and distance references. Witnesses may accurately describe what an ambiguous light looked like without accurately estimating how large or close its source was.

Each explanation has limits. Mine lights do not readily account for a clearly observed object departing through the sky. A planet does not land, obscure background features or illuminate nearby ground. An aircraft does not explain physical traces unless those traces were independently demonstrated. The problem is that the publicly available account does not preserve enough detail to determine which of these tests the observation actually passed.

The same reporting weakened the case in another sense by widening the claims without making the underlying documentation equally accessible. “Unidentified object”, “landing”, “extraterrestrial spacecraft” and “close encounter of the third kind” are not interchangeable descriptions. Until that happens, the investigation cannot be reproduced, and its most dramatic conclusions remain assertions rather than demonstrated findings.

## What the evidence supports about Heath Steele
The Heath Steele Mine story should not be dismissed simply because it concerns a UFO. A reported multi-witness event at a remote industrial site is historically relevant to New Brunswick, particularly when local people have continued to commemorate it. The witnesses may have encountered something genuinely puzzling, and the thin official record does not prove that nothing unusual occurred.

However, the case is weakly documented in the public domain. Its core may be a sincere sighting, but the available sources do not establish a landing, occupants or an extraterrestrial origin. The absence of accessible contemporary statements makes it impossible to know how closely later narratives match what was originally seen.

Within New Brunswick’s UFO history, Heath Steele is therefore best classified as an unresolved but poorly substantiated case family: memorable, locally persistent and potentially worth archival investigation, yet far less conclusive than its later titles suggest. What survives most clearly is not proof of a spacecraft landing, but an example of how an ambiguous event can acquire greater detail and certainty as it moves from witness memory into regional folklore.
