## Introduction

One of the most plausible natural explanations for the colour-changing light reported by a Nolinor Aviation Boeing 737 crew over northern Baffin Island in November 2018 is **atmospheric scintillation**. This is the familiar "twinkling" effect that makes bright stars appear to flash rapidly between white, red, green and blue, especially when they are low in the sky. The pilots' description of a bright light changing colour is therefore consistent with a well-understood optical phenomenon. However, the available public evidence is not detailed enough to show that scintillation was definitely responsible. The explanation remains plausible rather than proven because key observational details—such as the object's exact bearing, elevation and duration—were never released.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/pilots-report-ufo-sighting-over-nunavuts-northern-baffin-island/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nunatsiaq.com">[nunatsiaq.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nunatsiaq.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Pilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nunatsiaq NewsPilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin IslandNovember 27, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 2018</span></span></span>

## How stars can appear to change colour

Atmospheric scintillation occurs because starlight passes through constantly shifting pockets of air with different temperatures and densities. These layers bend the incoming light by tiny amounts, causing rapid fluctuations in brightness, apparent position and colour. The star itself is not changing colour; the Earth's atmosphere is altering how its light reaches the observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skyandtelescope.org/astronomy-resources/why-do-stars-twinkle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyandtelescope.org">[skyandtelescope.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyandtelescope.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sky &amp; Telescope Stellar Scintillation: Why Stars Twinkle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky &amp; TelescopeStellar Scintillation: Why Stars Twinkle - Sky &amp; Telescope...</span></span></span>

Bright stars are particularly striking because the human eye easily notices these rapid changes. Under poor viewing conditions, observers can see a sequence of flashes that appear red, green, white and occasionally blue within fractions of a second. This effect is strongest for objects close to the horizon because their light travels through a much greater thickness of atmosphere before reaching the observer. The World Meteorological Organization explicitly notes that scintillation can alter a distant light's apparent brightness, position and colour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/scintillation.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cloudatlas.wmo.int">[wmo.int]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cloudatlas.wmo.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Cloud Atlas Scintillation &#124; International Cloud Atlas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Cloud Atlas Scintillation &#124; International Cloud Atlas</span></span></span>

The colours reported publicly after the Baffin Island sighting—red, green and white, with some later accounts also mentioning blue—fall comfortably within the range commonly described for strong stellar scintillation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/pilots-report-ufo-sighting-over-nunavuts-northern-baffin-island/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nunatsiaq.com">[Nunatsiaq News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nunatsiaq.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Pilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nunatsiaq NewsPilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin IslandNovember 27, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 2018</span></span></span>

## Why Arctic night flying complicates visual judgement

The circumstances of the November [2018 sighting]({{ '2018-sighting/' | relative_url }}) make a scintillation explanation worthy of serious consideration without making it certain.

The aircraft was flying at night across northern Baffin Island, where there are few ground lights and very little visual reference outside the cockpit. In such conditions, pilots can accurately report what they see while still finding it difficult to judge a light's true distance or altitude. A single bright point against a dark sky provides few visual cues from which to estimate whether it is nearby or extremely distant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/pilots-report-ufo-sighting-over-nunavuts-northern-baffin-island/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nunatsiaq.com">[Nunatsiaq News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nunatsiaq.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Pilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nunatsiaq NewsPilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin IslandNovember 27, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 2018</span></span></span>

Cold Arctic conditions can also create strong temperature differences between layers of air. These gradients contribute to atmospheric turbulence that produces scintillation, although cold weather alone does not guarantee unusually strong colour changes. The important factor is the optical path through unstable air rather than temperature by itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skyandtelescope.org/astronomy-resources/why-do-stars-twinkle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyandtelescope.org">[skyandtelescope.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyandtelescope.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sky &amp; Telescope Stellar Scintillation: Why Stars Twinkle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky &amp; TelescopeStellar Scintillation: Why Stars Twinkle - Sky &amp; Telescope...</span></span></span>

An aircraft's own motion adds another complication. As the aircraft changes heading or position, a distant celestial object may appear to shift relative to the cockpit windows even though it is effectively stationary. Without precise measurements of angular movement, apparent motion alone is not enough to distinguish a star from a closer object.

## What observations would confirm or weaken this explanation

The principal weakness of the scintillation hypothesis is not the physics but the lack of detailed observational data.

Evidence that would strengthen the explanation includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the light remaining fixed against the background stars;
* a low elevation above the horizon;
* no independent radar or airborne target;
* an appearance lasting long enough for normal stellar observation;
* astronomical reconstruction showing a bright star or planet in the reported direction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skyandtelescope.org/astronomy-resources/why-do-stars-twinkle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyandtelescope.org">[skyandtelescope.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyandtelescope.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sky &amp; Telescope Stellar Scintillation: Why Stars Twinkle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky &amp; TelescopeStellar Scintillation: Why Stars Twinkle - Sky &amp; Telescope...</span></span></span>

</div>

Conversely, the explanation would become much less convincing if reliable evidence showed:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* sustained movement independent of the observing aircraft;
* measured changes in bearing inconsistent with a distant celestial object;
* corroborating radar returns from the same location;
* multiple independent observers reporting structured features rather than a point of light;
* instrument data demonstrating rapid manoeuvres.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/pilots-report-ufo-sighting-over-nunavuts-northern-baffin-island/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nunatsiaq.com">[Nunatsiaq News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nunatsiaq.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Pilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nunatsiaq NewsPilots report UFO sighting over Nunavut’s northern Baffin IslandNovember 27, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 2018</span></span></span>

</div>
