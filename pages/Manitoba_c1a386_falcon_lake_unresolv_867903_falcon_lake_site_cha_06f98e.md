## Introduction

The [Falcon Lake]({{ 'falcon-lake/' | relative_url }}) encounter is often described as one of Canada's best-documented UFO cases, but the investigation into the alleged landing site reveals why it has never been resolved. The central problem was not simply that investigators failed to find decisive evidence. It was that, by the time police, Royal Canadian Air Force (RCAF) personnel and other investigators were able to examine the location, the site's original condition had already been altered. Rain had fallen, visitors had entered the area, and Stefan Michalak himself had returned with an associate to recover objects and collect samples despite earlier requests not to disturb the scene. As a result, the [physical evidence]({{ 'physical-evidence/' | relative_url }}) lost much of its forensic value, leaving later investigators unable to determine with confidence whether the remaining traces supported, contradicted or had no connection to Michalak's account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-054.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2 - Canada.caMay 29, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2019</span></span></span>

## How officials searched for the location

The alleged landing site was not examined immediately after the reported encounter on 20 May 1967. Michalak was suffering from [burns]({{ 'burns/' | relative_url }}) and illness in the days that followed, and RCMP investigators noted that he was not initially able to guide them back to the exact location. Although aerial and ground searches were carried out soon after the incident, they failed to identify the precise site from his description alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-054.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2 - Canada.caMay 29, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2019</span></span></span>

## What was removed before the site was secured

One of the most important weaknesses in the Falcon Lake investigation concerns the handling of physical evidence before officials arrived.

According to RCMP records and later discussions based on those files, investigators had specifically asked Michalak that, if he rediscovered the location, he should leave everything untouched and notify them immediately. Instead, he returned with Gerald Hart, reportedly recovered a number of objects and removed material from the site before police and RCAF personnel were able to conduct their examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.experiencersupport.org/declassified/rcmp-ufo-report-regarding-landing-place-at-falcon-beach-manitoba-august" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: experiencersupport.org">[experiencersupport.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">experiencersupport.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rcmp ufo report regarding landing place at falcon beach manitoba august</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RCMP UFO Report: Regarding Landing Place at Falcon Beach, Manitoba – August 10 1967 &#124; The Experiencer Support Association...</span></span></span>

Items reportedly removed included:

* remnants of his burnt outer shirt;
* his steel measuring tape, which he said had been dropped during the incident;
* soil and moss samples;
* other small pieces of material considered potentially relevant to the encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.experiencersupport.org/declassified/rcmp-ufo-report-regarding-landing-place-at-falcon-beach-manitoba-august" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: experiencersupport.org">[experiencersupport.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">experiencersupport.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rcmp ufo report regarding landing place at falcon beach manitoba august</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RCMP UFO Report: Regarding Landing Place at Falcon Beach, Manitoba – August 10 1967 &#124; The Experiencer Support Association...</span></span></span>

This decision fundamentally changed the evidential situation. Instead of documenting the position of objects where they allegedly lay, investigators received items that had already been handled, transported and separated from their original context.

In forensic terms, context can be as important as the object itself. A burnt shirt found exactly where a witness claimed to have dropped it provides different evidential value from the same shirt recovered later without independent documentation of its original position.

## Why contamination and delay weakened the findings

By the time officials reached the alleged landing area, several independent factors had combined to weaken the reliability of any physical evidence.

First, the site had been exposed to the weather. Contemporary accounts note that rainfall occurred before the official examination, potentially washing away loose residues, disturbing surface soil and altering burn patterns or other fragile traces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-054.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2 - Canada.caMay 29, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2019</span></span></span>

Second, Michalak's removal of evidence meant investigators could no longer verify whether recovered objects genuinely originated where he said they had been found. This became especially important when radioactive samples entered the case.

Investigators later detected a small area of low-level radiation in a rock crevice and examined soil supplied by Michalak. However, because some samples had already been collected privately, handled directly and passed between different individuals before laboratory analysis, researchers could not establish a fully documented chain of custody. Library and Archives Canada researchers discussing the original files describe this as a compromised evidential record: later analysts could not always determine precisely where particular samples originated or whether they represented the same location examined by officials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-054.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2 - Canada.caMay 29, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2019</span></span></span>

The distinction is important. Radioactive material can only be interpreted confidently if investigators know exactly:

* where it was collected;
* who handled it;
* whether it was mixed with other material;
* whether contamination occurred after collection.

</div>

Those questions could not be answered conclusively in the Falcon Lake investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-054.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2 - Canada.caMay 29, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2019</span></span></span>

## What investigators actually found

Despite the compromised condition of the site, investigators did record several observations.

They described a roughly semicircular or circular area where moss appeared to have been disturbed or removed. ## Why the broken evidence chain still matters

The Falcon Lake case remains unusual because it generated more official documentation than many Canadian UFO reports. The RCMP, RCAF, medical authorities and civilian investigators all examined different aspects of the incident. Yet the investigation illustrates how an evidence chain can fail even when authorities become involved relatively quickly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-053.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 1 - Canada.caMay 15, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 15, 2019</span></span></span>

The critical weakness was not simply the absence of evidence. Rather, potentially important evidence was collected outside controlled conditions before investigators could document it. Every later interpretation—whether arguing that the physical traces supported Michalak's account or that they had ordinary explanations—has had to contend with that missing chain of custody.

For believers, this means potentially valuable evidence may have been lost before it could be properly examined. For sceptics, it means the surviving material cannot reliably demonstrate that an extraordinary event occurred because its provenance is uncertain. Both perspectives stem from the same investigative limitation.

Within [Manitoba]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-manitobas-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s UFO history, the Falcon Lake landing site therefore stands as a cautionary example. The case remains unresolved not because officials ignored it, but because the opportunity to examine the alleged landing area in its original condition had already passed before a systematic investigation could begin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/engage-learn/podcasts/discover/episode-054.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: canada.ca">[canada.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at LAC: The Falcon Lake incident, part 2 - Canada.caMay 29, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2019</span></span></span>
