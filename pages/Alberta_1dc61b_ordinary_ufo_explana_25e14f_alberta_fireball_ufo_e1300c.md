## Introduction

Bright fireballs are among the most dramatic sights in [Alberta]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-albertas-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s night and early morning skies, and they are also one of the most common natural events to be mistaken for UFOs. A large meteor entering the atmosphere can appear to fly unusually low, change colour, fragment into several glowing objects, or briefly illuminate the landscape. Without any obvious point of reference, witnesses often conclude that the object must have been much closer than it really was.

Within Alberta's UFO history, fireballs are especially important because many reports that initially describe an unknown craft are later matched with meteor observations, camera recordings and scientific trajectory reconstructions. The province's wide horizons, relatively dark skies outside its cities, and growing network of all-sky cameras provide unusually strong evidence for testing spectacular sightings against physical data rather than speculation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.amsmeteors.org/2021/02/bright-fireball-over-alberta-canada-on-february-22-2021/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amsmeteors.org">[American Meteor Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amsmeteors.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Meteor SocietyBright Fireball Over Alberta, Canada on February 22, 2021 - American Meteor SocietyFebruary 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why fireballs look low and nearby

A fireball is an exceptionally bright meteor, usually brighter than the planet Venus. Although it burns tens of kilometres above the Earth's surface, it often appears much closer because the human eye has few cues for judging distance against a dark sky.

Several characteristics make fireballs easy to mistake for unusual aircraft or unidentified objects:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Extreme brightness.** A fireball can briefly outshine every other object in the sky and even cast shadows.
* **Changing colours.** White, yellow, green and blue flashes are normal as different minerals vaporise and atmospheric conditions change.
* **Fragmentation.** Many meteoroids break apart, creating several glowing pieces that appear to fly in formation.
* **Apparent slowing or hovering.** Objects travelling almost directly towards an observer can seem to slow dramatically before crossing the sky.
* **Sudden disappearance.** Most fireballs extinguish abruptly once the meteoroid has burned away, giving the impression that an object has vanished.

</div>

Because Alberta's prairie landscape offers long, unobstructed views, observers may watch a fireball for several seconds across hundreds of kilometres of sky. That extended visibility can reinforce the mistaken impression that the object is flying locally rather than high in the upper atmosphere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.amsmeteors.org/2021/02/bright-fireball-over-alberta-canada-on-february-22-2021/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amsmeteors.org">[amsmeteors.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amsmeteors.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Meteor SocietyBright Fireball Over Alberta, Canada on February 22, 2021 - American Meteor SocietyFebruary 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

## How camera networks reconstruct their paths

One reason Alberta provides unusually good evidence is that many bright meteors are recorded simultaneously by multiple cameras.

When several recordings exist, researchers compare the exact time, viewing direction and elevation from each location. Using triangulation, they can reconstruct:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the meteor's atmospheric trajectory;
* its entry speed;
* the altitude at which it fragmented;
* whether any meteorites were likely to survive to the ground.

</div>

This approach replaces eyewitness estimates—which are often unreliable for distance and speed—with measurable geometry. Modern meteor camera networks routinely demonstrate that objects perceived as flying just above nearby houses or fields were actually travelling 70–100 kilometres above the ground.

Scientific reconstruction also allows investigators to distinguish meteors from aircraft, satellites or re-entering space debris. Aircraft move far more slowly and follow controlled flight paths, while satellites generally cross the sky steadily without the sudden flares and fragmentation typical of large fireballs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11883" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arxiv.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fireball streak detection with minimal CPU processing requirements for the Desert Fireball Network data processing pipelineSeptember...</span></span></span>

## What the 2021 Alberta events revealed

One of Alberta's clearest modern examples occurred on **22 February 2021**, when an exceptionally bright morning fireball was seen across much of the province shortly after 6:20 a.m.

The event generated hundreds of witness reports from Alberta, with additional observations from neighbouring provinces and even Montana. Doorbell cameras, dashboard cameras and security systems captured the fireball from numerous angles, allowing investigators to compare independent recordings rather than relying solely on memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.amsmeteors.org/2021/02/bright-fireball-over-alberta-canada-on-february-22-2021/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amsmeteors.org">[American Meteor Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amsmeteors.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Meteor SocietyBright Fireball Over Alberta, Canada on February 22, 2021 - American Meteor SocietyFebruary 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

Many initial witnesses described the object as unusually low, extremely bright or unlike any ordinary aircraft. Some questioned whether they had seen a spacecraft or another unexplained aerial object. As additional evidence accumulated, however, the picture became much clearer.

Researchers and meteor organisations combined the eyewitness reports with video evidence to estimate a trajectory passing north of Edmonton. The consistency between hundreds of independent observations strongly supported the conclusion that the event was a natural meteor rather than an unidentified craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.amsmeteors.org/2021/02/bright-fireball-over-alberta-canada-on-february-22-2021/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amsmeteors.org">[American Meteor Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amsmeteors.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Meteor SocietyBright Fireball Over Alberta, Canada on February 22, 2021 - American Meteor SocietyFebruary 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

The episode illustrates an important pattern in Alberta's UFO history: spectacular reports often become significantly less mysterious once multiple recordings are assembled and analysed scientifically.

## Why Alberta is well suited to testing dramatic reports

Alberta offers several practical advantages for investigating reports of brilliant lights.

Large areas of open countryside provide long sightlines that allow the same event to be seen simultaneously from communities separated by hundreds of kilometres. Increasing numbers of home security cameras, dashboard cameras and dedicated astronomical cameras also mean that many bright fireballs are independently recorded.

Because multiple observers are looking at the same object from different positions, investigators can compare perspectives rather than depending on a single witness. This makes Alberta one of the better places in Canada for separating genuinely unexplained reports from natural atmospheric events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.amsmeteors.org/2021/02/bright-fireball-over-alberta-canada-on-february-22-2021/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amsmeteors.org">[American Meteor Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amsmeteors.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Meteor SocietyBright Fireball Over Alberta, Canada on February 22, 2021 - American Meteor SocietyFebruary 23, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

## Distinguishing a fireball from other reported UFOs

Although not every unusual light is a meteor, several clues point towards a fireball rather than an unidentified craft:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* It appears suddenly without warning.
* It crosses a large section of the sky in only a few seconds.
* It becomes brighter before fading rapidly.
* It may fragment into several glowing pieces.
* Many observers over a very wide geographical area report seeing the same event at nearly the same time.

</div>

By contrast, reports involving lights that remain visible for many minutes, hover, repeatedly change direction or return on different nights usually require different [explanations]({{ 'explanations/' | relative_url }}), such as aircraft, satellites, drones or astronomical objects.

The Sky Canada Project also stresses that an object being reported as "unidentified" does not imply an extraterrestrial origin. Careful comparison with astronomical, aviation and atmospheric data resolves many sightings that initially appear extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ised-isde.canada.ca">[ISED Canada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ised-isde.canada.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on canada.ca.</span></span></span>

## What fireballs contribute to Alberta's UFO history

Fireballs occupy an unusual place in Alberta's record of unexplained aerial sightings. They generate some of the province's most dramatic eyewitness accounts, yet they also provide some of its strongest examples of scientific resolution.

Events such as the widely observed February 2021 fireball demonstrate how initial uncertainty can give way to a well-supported explanation once multiple witnesses, camera footage and trajectory analysis are combined. Rather than diminishing the significance of these sightings, such investigations show how modern observational networks help distinguish genuinely unexplained reports from spectacular but entirely natural events, making fireballs an essential part of understanding Alberta's broader UFO history.

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
